# Sans Fiat — Game Design Document

Oct 17, 2026 · @Alan · updated Oct 20, 2026 to the proof of concept as built (after the integration and polish passes)

## How to read this document

This document holds the vision, the structure of a game, and the status of every mechanic. The exact rules and numbers
of what is built are in two companion files:

- [mechanics/economy.md](mechanics/economy.md): map, clans, markets, pre-game and founding, civ store, labour, projects,
  trading, expansion, years, leaning, discoveries, victory.
- [mechanics/military.md](mechanics/military.md): scouts, trails and fog; armies; supply, porters and caches.

Each mechanic is marked **built** (in the proof of concept and tested), **partial** (in, but simpler than designed) or
**not built**. A **decision** records a choice the code made where this document was open or said something else, and
why. Constant names (`CIV.levy`, `ARMY.size`) refer to the tuning files listed in `CLAUDE.md`.

## Vision and pillars

Sans Fiat ("without fiat money") is a turn-based hex 4X set at the dawn of farming. It has no treasury: nothing is bought
with gold from the player's coffers, and a good is worth only what the people in the world will trade for it. The era
runs from the last hunter-gatherers to the first cities, through the spread of crops and tamed animals.

The game keeps the genre's familiar loop of explore, expand, exploit and exterminate. It replaces the usual abstractions
with simulated people, food and distance.

| Pillar | What it means in play | Where it was tested |
| --- | --- | --- |
| No money | Goods are physical, sit in a place, spoil, and must be carried. Value comes from trade at markets, never from a price list. Anything that acts like money (shells, beads, clay tokens) is made by someone and traded. | hex-economy |
| People are the economy | Clans eat, grow, split, move and trade on their own. The player steers their own people and persuades others; nobody else is a puppet. | hex-economy |
| Distance is the cost | Every action spends labour, and labour drains with distance from food. Logistics, not hard caps, limits how far an empire or an army reaches. | supply-lines, scout |
| Ground is held by people | Territory is held only while someone stands on it or works it. Holding ground costs troops or settlers. | paint-army |
| The map is earned | The true cost of terrain is unknown until scouted, and routes improve as trails and bridges are built. Infrastructure is shared by everyone. | scout |
| One world, many starts | The map and its people are generated by a simulated pre-game, so every start sits in a history that explains it. | hex-economy |

The source material is the five sandbox prototypes and the design notes in `docs/notes/`.

## Game structure

A game has three acts: a pre-game the player watches, a founding where it takes a civ, and the main game it plays.
A turn stands for a full round of seasons. **Built.**

1. **Pre-game (simulated, 170–200 turns on seeds 1–3).** Three seed clans spread over an empty map, hold land, use it up,
   split and trade at gatherings. The player watches it at speed or skips it (headless, about 0.5–1 s). It ends, from
   turn 100, once there is a founding site per player: a thriving market with a domesticated hearth in reach, markets and
   hearths 8 apart, all on one landmass; at turn 500 it ends regardless.
2. **Founding.** Each site becomes a civ; the player picks which to lead, or watches all AI. A civ's first people are the
   clans living within its market's reach, and the market tile is its capital. Other gatherings become permanent markets.
   All other clans stay unaligned and peaceful.
3. **Main game.** Two to four civs (one human) expand, explore, build, trade and fight until conquest or turn 150.
   Unaligned clans keep living by the pre-game's rules, so the world keeps moving around the players.

**Decision (founding):** sites must share a landmass (counting land a trail or bridge would open) so every civ can reach
every other; the design said only "8 tiles apart with domesticated grain in reach".

### Turn order

One `endTurn` runs these phases in order. In each, module steps marked `before` run, then the core step, then the rest.
Phases 1–6 also run in the pre-game; module steps run in the main game only. **Built.**

| Phase | What happens |
| --- | --- |
| 1. regrow | Year roll per region (civ), then tiles regrow (skipping fallow tiles, × the year) |
| 2. forage | Civs draft workers from their clans; clans move, adjust land, decide to go to market, harvest; drafted workers build, craft, hunt, farm, gather materials |
| 3. trade | Civs post goods above their reserve; every market clears; civs collect; leaning from trade; scouts' knowledge passes between civs at shared markets |
| 4. settle | Clans eat, grow, starve, divide, split; civ store spoils, levy, cap, site expiry, new cities |
| 5. occupy | Held grain tames, abandoned grain goes feral |
| 6. markets | Markets fade and form; leaning decays; clans join civs |
| 7. orders | AI civs' actions (all modules); scouts work their routes; porters run |
| 8. armies | Zones spread, skirmish, hold, capture; armies eat, resupply on auto, starve; caches spoil and change hands |
| 9. discoveries | Discoveries and spread by contact; victory; fog update |

The player's own actions apply immediately when given, between turns; only AI orders run inside the turn.

## Map and terrain

**Built.** Pointy-top axial hex grid, 44 × 28. Terrain decides which foods a tile carries and how hard it is to cross.

| Terrain | Move cost (labour) | Wild food cap | Trail brings it to | Notes |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Plains | 1 | game 3, grain 4 | — | Best grain land; where grain is first domesticated |
| Forest | 2 | game 3, fruit 4 | 1 or 2 (hidden) | Yields wood |
| Hills | 2 | game 2, fruit 1 | 1 or 2 (hidden) | Yields stone |
| Desert | 1 | none | — | Crossable, feeds no one |
| Mountain | impassable | none | 3 if bridgeable (hidden span) | 2 in 10 have a span of 1 or 2; only span 1 can be bridged |
| Ocean | impassable | fish 4, shellfish 4 in shallows | — | |

Features add food: a river adds fish 5 and grain 1 (and yields clay); a coast adds shellfish 1. Fertility varies in
patches from 0.4× to 2×. Generation: height noise, inlets, inland seas, mountain ridges with hilly flanks and rare passes,
deserts (about 20% of land), rivers, coast and shallows.

- **Decision:** hills cost 2 (the table said 2–3).
- **Terrain defence: built, decision.** Forest (+25%) and hills (+50%) help only troops dug in for at least a turn,
  and garrisons; troops on the move get nothing from the ground.
- **Hidden cost: built** for the whole map (forest, hills, mountains), revealed per civ by scouts.
- **Fog of war: built**, opaque (see Exploration).

## People and settlements

### Clans (from hex-economy) — built

Diet of one food per person per turn with a variety rule (a food counts in full up to 40% of need, 30% beyond), 30%
spoilage, land capped at roam cost 6, growth 3–7% when fed, shrinking below 80% fed, dividing at 12, a starving clan of
8 sending a daughter, moving on after 3 starving turns, grain taming over 15 + 120 turns to 2.5× yield. Details in
[economy.md](mechanics/economy.md#clans).

### Civ settlements — built

- **City. Decision:** a city has no population of its own. The capital rises on the founding market; later cities are
  founded by a settled civ clan (6+ people, 5+ turns settled) next to grain tamed at least 30%, 3+ tiles from any city,
  at most 12. A city works radius 1 plus its civ's clan land within 2, holds territory within 2, and adds 50 to the
  civ's store cap.
- **Expansion replaces the settler. Built.** A civ grows when its clans divide. The player may choose where the next
  daughter goes (`setDaughterSite`) and where the next city goes (`setCitySite`: daughters are steered there and the city
  rises when a civ clan settles next to it). Choices lapse after 20 and 40 turns. Otherwise daughters pick the best site
  spaced from cities. **Partial:** only daughters that walk off are steered; a clan halving its land is not.
- **Joining. Built** through leaning (below).
- **Unaligned clans stay peaceful. Built:** there is no action that attacks a clan.

## Economy without money

The player never holds a balance. Everything a civ has is a physical good in its store; a good's value is the ratio
the last market cleared at, and that ratio is nobody's property.

### Markets (from hex-economy) — built

Baskets valued at Σ√amount; up to 300 iterations of price search; exact rationing; ratios relative with a geometric mean
of 1. A clan goes to market only if the gain beats the trip (5% of labour per unit of path cost, reach 3). Gatherings form
where 4+ clans are in reach and fade when smoothed volume falls under 2. **Decision:** in the main game neutral markets
keep forming (on the longest-held site) and fading, but not within 5 of a market that fell quiet the same turn; a civ's
founding market never fades. **Decision: civs buy only what they lack.** A civ takes in exchange only goods it is not
posting itself, so it never buys back its own surplus.

### Goods — partial

| Kind | Goods | Status |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Food | game, grain, fruit, fish, shellfish | Built |
| Material | wood, stone, clay | Built (from forest, hills, river tiles). Flint and hides not built |
| Craft | pottery | Built (from clay once pottery is known). Tools and cloth not built |
| Token | shell beads | Not built |

### Production — built

- **Decision: one pooled store per civ** (`civ.store`), capped at 50 a city + 80 a storehouse; past the cap materials
  rot first, then food. Fed member clans hand half their leftover food to it (the levy).
- **Decision: founding endowment.** Each civ's store starts the main game with 30 grain (`CIV.endowment`), since its
  clans' stores are empty at founding; without it the first gifts, porters and army provisions are unaffordable.
- **Decision: drafted labour.** Labour is people. Each turn a civ drafts up to 25% of each non-starving clan, as many as
  its cities need (project 3, farming 2, hunting 1, crafting 1, one per material below its target). Drafted people eat
  but do not forage, so labour spent on the civ is food the clans don't gather. Starving clans are spared.
- **A project costs labour plus goods.** Goods are paid from the store when started.

| Project | Labour | Goods | Effect | Status |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Granary | 10 | wood 4, clay 2 | Food spoilage × 0.3 (store, and clans within 3) | Built |
| Storehouse | 12 | wood 6, stone 2 | Store cap +80 | Built |
| Wall | 15 | stone 6, wood 2 | City defenders × 1.5, garrison +2 | Built |
| Workshop, shrine | | | | Not built |

- **The player gets goods by trading surplus. Built.** Everything above the civ's reserve is posted at its market and at
  one other within 12 (chosen for the materials it lacks). **Decision: posting reserves.** By default a civ keeps 8% of
  its cap of each food, 5% of crafts, and what its next projects need of each material; `setPosting` overrides a good's
  reserve. **Decision:** clans trading where a civ posts bring a little wood, stone and clay from their land to sell,
  so a civ can trade food for materials it cannot gather.
- **City supply funds armies. Built.** Armies and porters draw food from the same store.

Every flow is physical: land → clan harvest → levy and drafted work → store → market, projects, armies. Labour spent on
troops or projects is labour not spent on food, which is the central trade-off.

## Exploration and infrastructure — built

Full rules in [military.md](mechanics/military.md#scouts-trails-and-fog).

- **Hidden cost. Built** as designed.
- **Scout routes. Built. Decision: reveal, then trail, then walk.** A route from A to B reveals unknown tiles on the
  optimistic path (1 labour each) until it cannot beat the known one, builds trails along the best known route
  (2 forest, 3 hills, 6 mountain labour), then walks to B. Bridges span 1. Each civ starts with a scout and may have 3.
- **Shared infrastructure. Built.** Trails help every civ, clan, army and porter, and widen market reach.
- **Map memory. Built.** Revealed costs belong to the civ that scouted them; a revealed tile can pass to a civ whose
  clans trade at the same market (5% a turn per pair).
- **Decision: AI roads.** An AI civ's first scout trails roads from its capital toward rival cities, then its own.
- **Fog of war. Built. Decision: opaque fog.** A civ sees around its units (2), cities (3), clans (1) and its clans' land,
  and remembers what it has seen. Unseen tiles hide everything, including in panels and debug views.
- **Gap, decision:** scouts cost nothing to raise.

## Military — built

Full rules in [military.md](mechanics/military.md#armies).

- **Raising. Decision:** an army is `ARMY.size` 10 people taken from the civ's clans (which need 15+ people), provisioned
  with up to 4 food a troop from the store. The army panel has one "Raise army" row with a button per city. **Decision: captain cap and disband.** At most 3 captains; an army within 2 of
  an own city may disband, and its troops rejoin the nearest clan. Unit scale is settled: a troop is a person.
- **Zones, spread, deployed and undeployed, entrenchment (+25% a turn to +75%), retreat (3 to 1, 60%), skirmish per tile
  (half of enemy power):** built as designed.
- **Cut-off detachments. Built:** walk back to the captain, or are promoted by the player (under the cap); after 8 turns
  cut off they go home. **Decision:** the link to a captain is a connected zone path.
- **Territory. Built:** `territoryOf` = troops on the tile, else a fort, else the nearest city within 2.
- **Forts. Built:** 2 labour, 2 wood, 2 stone; garrison 4; troops on it fully dug in; captured with the tile. Not on a
  city tile (a city has its own garrison).
- **Decision: city garrison, walls and sieges.** A city's garrison is 4, +1 per 12 people of its civ's clans within 2
  (at most +3), +2 with a wall; it regrows 1 a turn while nobody holds the tile. It defends when no own troops stand
  there, each troop at 1.1 power (× 1.5 with a wall, × the terrain). Own troops in a city fight × 1.5 (× 2.25 with a
  wall). A siege kills at most 2 of the garrison a turn, so taking a city takes a few turns and the defender is
  warned. A city falls when an enemy holds its tile with the garrison at 0; it is renamed for its new owner, and its
  clans stay with their civ. A civ left with no city is fallen and its clans become unaligned.
- **Decision: hungry armies cannot attack**, and defend at half power.
- **AI. Built:** raises with people, food and a reachable rival, but not in the first 20 turns after founding
  (**decision: grace period**, `ARMY.aiGrace`; without it AI armies hit some players by turn 5); marches as one body on
  the cheapest beatable rival city; defends threatened cities; builds up to 2 forts near home facing a rival city;
  disbands starving armies at home.
- **Warnings. Built:** the player is alerted when a rival raises an army within 15 of one of its cities, when enemy
  troops come within 3 of a city, and every siege turn.

## Supply and logistics — built

Full rules in [military.md](mechanics/military.md#supply).

- **Labour.** Every unit has 4 labour a turn; moving, building, resupplying, foraging and pillaging draw on it.
- **Food store. Partial.** The captain carries the army's food (4 a troop); it eats 0.25 a troop a turn and 0.25 more per
  troop that fought. **Decision: attrition on the army total.** A hungry army loses 10% of all its troops a turn, spread
  over its detachments. **Healing: built.** A fed army that did not fight regains 1 troop a turn, up to the troops it was
  raised or promoted with (`troopsMax`).
- **Supply line. Built:** to the cheapest own city (with food in store) or cache within path cost 10, not through enemy
  territory; resupply costs 1 + cost/3 labour. The army panel shows the line.
- **Forage and pillage. Built. Decision: `landOwner` decides** (fort, city territory, else the claiming clan's civ), not
  who stands on the tile. Forage 2 labour on own or neutral land, pillage 1 on enemy land, 4 food; the tile then yields
  nothing for 5 turns.
- **Automation. Partial:** auto-supply per captain is built; auto-forage and a labour reserve are not (the AI forages).
- **Porters and caches. Built.** Porters carry 12, eat 1 a turn while loaded, feed armies or stock caches on standing
  routes with a target level. **Decision: porters refuse enemy land** and turn back when their target turns hostile.
  Caches spoil 30% a turn and change hands with the tile. A porter that turns back returns the same foods it loaded.
- **Lines carry goods. Not built:** projects draw goods from the pooled store.
- **Pack animals. Not built.**

## Standard 4X elements

| 4X element | In Sans Fiat | Status |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Explore | Scouts reveal hidden cost and build shared trails | Built |
| Expand | Clans divide; the player picks daughter and city sites; unaligned clans join | Built |
| Exploit | Tiles yield foods and materials; drafted people work; surplus traded at markets | Built |
| Exterminate | Captains paint zones; skirmish per tile; supply limits reach | Built |
| Production queue | One labour-and-goods project per city | Built |
| Buildings | Granary, storehouse, wall | Partial (no workshop, shrine) |
| Technology | Discoveries from practice | Partial (pottery, herding) |
| Diplomacy | Trade at shared markets, gifts, joining clans | Partial (no civ treaties) |
| AI rivals | Same actions as the player, chosen by each module's `ai` | Built |
| Victory | Conquest or prosperity | Built |

### Good and bad years — built

Each 8 × 8 region has a 2% chance a turn of a drought (× 0.5) or a bumper year (× 1.5) for 3–5 turns, scaling regrowth
and materials. Droughts in the player's land raise an alert.

### Clans are the prize — built

Each unaligned clan has a leaning toward each civ, from trade at markets where the civ posts and from food gifts.
**Decision: gifts capped with diminishing leaning.** At most 10 food per clan per civ per turn; each unit buys less, and
starving clans value it 5 times as much as fed ones. Leaning decays 3% a turn; at 1 the clan joins its strongest civ.
**Partial:** the civ panel lists leaning clans and the strongest rival; the map does not draw leanings.

### Discoveries — partial

No research pool; a discovery comes from doing the thing.

- **Agriculture: built.** Every civ starts with it (founding needs a domesticated hearth).
- **Pottery: built.** 20 turns of practice. A turn digging clay counts 1; a turn holding clay got any other way (trade)
  counts 0.5, so a civ without a river can learn it too. Spoilage × 0.7 for the store and every member clan; clay can
  be crafted into pottery. A civ keeps 4 clay back from market.
- **Herding: partial.** 25 turns of practice. A turn hunting near a city counts 1; a turn in which member clans harvest
  2+ game counts 0.5. Hunters bring 1.5× game and deplete herds half as fast; herds do not move with their people.
- **Spread by contact: built.** +0.5 practice a turn per civ in trading contact that knows it.
- **Weaving, stoneworking: not built.**

### Diplomacy

- **With clans: built** as leaning (above).
- **With civs: partial.** Civs trade at shared markets (a civ may post at a rival's market) and so spread discoveries and
  map knowledge. Truce, passage and shared-market treaties are not built.
- **No tribute in gold:** holds by construction.

### Victory — built

- **Conquest:** one civ owns every founding city.
- **Prosperity. Decision:** after 150 main-game turns, the civ still holding a city with the highest people now / people
  at founding wins. The design said "most people", but founding hands the richest hearth the most people, so a head
  count rewards the start; growth since founding rewards the play.

### Making it readable — built

1. **Show why things happen. Built.** Every rule that acts on an entity records a reason (`note`); the side panel shows a
   clan's, market's, city's or unit's last 8. Events that concern the player are alerts (lines starting `!`), including
   rival armies raised nearby, troops approaching a city and sieges. The in-game help and `docs/guide.html` say how to
   defend.
2. **Good and bad years. Built** (above).
3. **Clans are the prize. Built** (above).
4. **Pick your start. Built.** The founding panel compares the candidate civs.

## Proof-of-concept scope

The proof of concept is one playable game: a simulated pre-game, then a human civ against 1–3 AI civs, played to a
conquest or prosperity win on a 44 × 28 map. It should answer one question: is a 4X with no treasury fun to steer?

### Built

- Pre-game from hex-economy, run headless or animated, with debug map views; ends in founding sites per player
- Founding: the player chooses a civ or watches all AI; other gatherings become permanent markets
- Civ clans, cities, drafted labour, expansion by chosen daughter and city sites, clans joining by leaning
- Markets with 5 foods, 3 materials (wood, stone, clay) and pottery; civ posting with reserves
- Projects: granary, storehouse, wall
- Captains, painted zones, skirmish, entrenchment, terrain defence, retreat, forts (AI too), garrisons, sieges, city
  capture, healing
- Supply lines, forage and pillage, auto-supply, porters, caches
- Scouts, hidden cost, trails and bridges, opaque fog, map knowledge spread at markets
- Discoveries: pottery and herding (agriculture from the start)
- Good and bad years, leaning and gifts, the "why" log, alerts
- Conquest and prosperity victories; AI civs on the same action set
- Replay: a seed plus the action list reproduces a game exactly (`game.replay()`)

### Partial

- Herding (no moving herds); automation (auto-supply only); goods (no flint, hides, tools, cloth); leanings not on the map

### Out (for later)

- Shell-bead tokens, cloth, weaving, stoneworking and the rest of the discovery list
- Workshop and shrine; goods carried along supply lines; pack animals
- Civ-to-civ treaties (truce, passage, shared market)
- Attacking unaligned clans; clans changing sides when their city is captured
- Per-unit labour reserves for automation; a cost for scouts
- Rivers and sea as transport
- Save/load beyond the seed-plus-action replay

### Architecture

- Plain ES modules, no build step, no dependencies; `npm start`, `npm test` (node --test), `npm run sim`, `npm run bench`.
- `dispatch(state, action) -> {state, error?}` is pure. Every action has `validate`, `apply` and `candidates`, so the AI,
  tests and UI use the same entry point.
- State is plain JSON. All randomness goes through `rand(state)`, so a seed plus the action list replays exactly.
- Core (`src/clans.js`, `src/market.js`, `src/rules.js`) runs the clans, markets and phases; features live in four
  modules (`civ`, `scout`, `army`, `supply`) wired through a registry. Only `render.js`, `main.js`, `views.js` and module
  `ui.js` files touch the DOM. See `CLAUDE.md` for the contract.

### Milestones

All six are reached: base, founding, economy, exploration, war, rivals and victory. `npm run sim` plays a full game on a
fixed seed with every civ on the AI.

## Open questions and risks

The biggest risk is that a game with no balance to spend feels like watching, not steering. The player's levers are:
where daughters and cities go, what to keep back from market, what to build, whom to feed, where to scout, and where
to paint.

### Settled by the proof of concept

- [x] Should domesticated grain be required near a founding market? Yes: a hearth in reach is required.
- [x] What links a detachment to its captain? A connected path through the zone.
- [x] Can enemy units cut a supply line or attack porters? Only by holding the tiles the line runs through.
- [x] How do auto-supply and labour share? Auto-supply takes the labour it needs first; the AI keeps labour for food by
  walking at most 2 a turn.
- [x] Unit scale: troops are people taken from clans and returned on disbanding.
- [x] Prosperity measure: growth since founding, not a head count.
- [x] Pre-game length: 170–200 turns, 0.5–1 s headless.

### Still open

- [ ] About 20% of clans are starving at game start. Is that the right pressure?
- [ ] Should grain tame only while its holder is growing?
- [ ] Should daughters trade on better terms with parent clans, a kinship network?
- [ ] How strong should entrenchment and retreat be?
- [ ] **Labour as the only cost.** Does labour plus goods give enough meaningful choices, or does it collapse into "more
  food is always right"?
- [ ] **Joining rule.** Are gifts and trade a lever the player can actually pull against AI civs?
- [ ] **Performance with the UI.** The animated fast-forward clones state every turn and runs about twice as slow as
  headless.
- [ ] **Captured cities.** Should a captured city's clans, or its market, change sides? (Now: the city is renamed for
  its new owner; its clans stay, and go unaligned only when their civ has no city left.)
- [ ] **Early war.** Is a 20-turn grace before AI armies enough for a new player to raise a defence?
