# M3: A game

What the M2 retro ([m2-retro.md](m2-retro.md)) leads to. M2 built the systems and left a
frozen world with nothing to decide. M3 makes it a game: land you claim and guard, armies
that take it and lose it, a rival that plays for the same goal, a way to win and to lose,
and a page that is the game. Under it, the engine takes the shape all of that needs, and a
judge and a player run on every change.

The Relay and process changes that go with it are in [m3-relay.md](m3-relay.md). The
visions the pieces build to are `docs/design/war.md`, `technology.md` and `page.md`, drafted
from your stories for you to correct. The scores every piece is judged on are in
[`docs/process.md`](../process.md).

## Your four answers

The retro asked four questions. These are the answers the pieces below assume; change any
and the pieces change with it.

1. **A civ's land is what it claims and guards** (`war.md`). The 2-step ring goes.
2. **Clans and people may leave a civ.** Standing has to be losable or nothing is at stake.
   A civ clan that leans more to another civ than its own for long enough leaves to it, by
   the same rule it joined by.
3. **The first way to win: the last capital standing, or the most Standing at the turn
   limit.** The way to lose: your capital falls. Influence alone can't end a game; conquest
   alone makes a game that's over when nobody's reached anybody.
4. **Every unit marches at one person's pace.** The camp's rule; the scout takes it.

## 1. Foundation

Two pieces that change no rule and that everything after stands on. They run first, in
either order.

### H. The engine's shape for many units and players

**Goal:** The engine's shape matches the game it's becoming: many units per civ, of kinds;
orders addressed to a civ and a unit, which the player, the AI and a script give through
one door; and what every actor shares living on the actor. Nothing a player sees changes,
and the pinned digests prove it.

**Spec:** sonnet high: settled refactor work, and the probe for a cheaper designer

**Spec-review:** opus medium

**Shape:** Henry Ford: every change is settled here and the digests are the proof. It
splits into the units module, the orders, and the rest.

**Design:** docs/design/actors.md, docs/architecture.md

**Decided:**
- `units.rs` becomes `units/{mod,camp,scout,line,ai}.rs`. `Unit` keeps what every unit has
  and holds its kind's own fields in a `UnitState`; the page's view JSON keeps its shape.
- Spoiling, eating from a store and walking along a path are written once each, on `Actor`
  in `actors.rs`, and clans, the civ's store, the camp and the scout use them. Each keeps its
  own rule for what hunger does. The civ becomes an actor whose store is its store.
- `World::order(civ, Order)` validates and applies an order for any civ. Orders name their
  unit: move, zone, switches, resupply, forage, pillage, route, disband; raise names the civ.
  The player's orders go through `act` and are logged; the AI's go through `order` and are
  re-derived on replay. `SAVE_VERSION` bumps for the new log shape; the world digests don't
  move.
- `lead_orders` moves into core as a script the wasm build also runs, so `cross-build.mjs`
  calls it instead of mirroring it.
- One event constructor; `Event`, `EventKind` and their wording move to `events.rs`.
- Basket helpers sit beside `Basket`; one tile-distance helper; colliding names renamed
  (`land::hold`, `Tile.held`/`unheld`, `map::Zone`, `clans::divide`, `units::walk`).
- Shared test fixtures in `tests/common`. `cli/src/main.rs` splits into a module per command.
- `engine/CLAUDE.md`: one row per file, one clause each, and "shared actor behaviour goes in
  `actors.rs`" under Adding a mechanic.
- `stats::digest` is unchanged for every seed and size; `./check.sh` passes, cross-build
  included.

**In scope:** core, the CLI's split, `web`'s view and order calls, the page's order calls,
`cross-build.mjs`, tests, `engine/CLAUDE.md`.

**Out of scope:** any rule or number; more than one camp per civ; unit kinds; goods other
than food.

### G. The game judges itself

**Goal:** Every change is judged on the scores that matter, on the retro maps, against a
control, in CI, so no piece can quietly undo another as #75 did; and anyone, human or
Claude, can play a civ turn by turn from a terminal and read what a player sees. The
question it answers: can a session play a led civ for a hundred turns and say what it
couldn't tell?

**Spec:** opus medium: tooling every later piece leans on, and a prompt pair to design

**Spec-review:** opus medium

**Shape:** Easel and Canvas: the judge, the text view, the order loop and the score line are
the easel. The canvas sets each band from what main does, writes the player and judge
prompts, plays one game with a model as player, and records what it cost and found.

**Design:** docs/design/vision.md, docs/process.md

**Decided:**
- **Standing** is computed each turn per civ: its clans' people over the world's, and once
  land exists (L), the people on its land too. `stats::summary` carries it; the page's top
  bar shows it (P).
- `sansfiat sweep --judge` runs the seven retro maps with every civ on the AI and reports,
  per map and in total: the five scores (`process.md`): Standing per civ at +100 and +200,
  lead changes, spread; AI orders per hundred turns; people, land and clans changing hands
  after the pick; and health: the pre-game ends by centres, camps kept, units starved
  against the `--no-army` control, no panic, digests stable across two runs. `--json` prints
  it machine-readable.
- Bands live in `engine/judge.toml`: a failing band fails CI, a warning band prints. The
  canvas sets them from main. Each later piece adds its check and moves the bands it means
  to move, saying so in its PR.
- `sansfiat play --seed N --size S --civ K` reads one JSON order or `end N` a line from stdin
  and prints a compact text view after each: turn, Standing of every civ, the store and
  keep, each unit with its pack, line and hunger, the civ's clans and the unaligned ones in
  reach with their leanings, the feed since the last call with alerts, and the legal orders.
  `why ID` prints a subject's reasons. Orders go through the same validation as the page.
- `sansfiat judge` runs the sweep's judge over a `play` log: the score line over time and
  what the player did.
- `relay/players/player.md` and `judge.md`: a player prompt that plays for Standing and
  writes what it meant to do, what it did and what surprised it each decision; a judge prompt
  that reads the log and lists problems only: decisions that had no real choice, reasons
  missing or wrong, orders with no visible result, turns where it did nothing. Praise isn't a
  finding.
- The canvas plays one game on Sonnet and judges it, and records cost, minutes, points and
  findings in `relay/specs/<n>/play.md`.
- Root `CLAUDE.md` gains the judge and play lines under Checks.

**In scope:** Standing, the judge, the bands file, CI, the play loop and text view, the
judge over a log, the prompts, one trial game and its record.

**Out of scope:** a Relay play step (m3-relay.md, once the trial has paid); strategy files;
driving the page; any rule change.

## 2. The game

Four pieces, in this order: L, P, A, R. Each is a system the player meets, cut into builds by
area, with one question in the scores.

### L. A civ's land

**Goal:** A civ's land is what it claims and can hold, and the people on it are what it
plays for. The player paints a claim out from the capital; the civ's guard spreads over it
as every actor divides its labour, so a wide claim is thin; clans whose homes are on it lean
to the civ, pay its levy at its market, and are protected; clans may leave as they join. The
2-step ring, the dead work circles and the market ring go. With them go the things that
stopped the land feeling right in M2: festivals that flip, trails under every worked tile
and none under a home, desert as cheap as plains. The question: on the retro maps, does a
civ that claims and guards gain Standing over one that doesn't, and does the lead change
hands after the pick?

**Spec:** opus medium: a new mechanic the player meets, and the first that counts toward
Standing

**Spec-review:** opus medium

**Shape:** Easel and Canvas: the claim, the guard, leaning by land, leaving and the page's
border are the easel; the trail and festival rules are a second easel. The canvas is whether
claiming pays and the lead moves, tuned against the pre-game still ending by centres, which
G checks.

**Design:** docs/design/war.md, docs/design/founding.md, docs/design/clans.md,
docs/design/supply.md, docs/design/map.md

**Decided:**
- A claim is a connected set of tiles including the capital, painted and unpainted on the
  page as the camp's zone is, by the player or the AI. Claiming costs nothing; a civ can
  claim land it can't hold.
- The **guard** is people the civ raises as it raises warriors, who stay on its land. The
  civ divides their guarding over the claim as the camp divides over its zone, so a tile's
  guard thins as the claim grows. A rival unit entering a tile whose guard is below a floor
  takes it from the claim.
- A clan whose home is on a civ's land gains leaning to that civ each turn, more under a
  thicker guard. Leaning from gifts and trade stays. Joining stays at the threshold.
- A civ clan whose leaning to another civ exceeds its own civ's for a run of turns leaves to
  it, its daughters with it. Standing counts a civ's clans wherever they live, and the
  people of unaligned clans whose homes are on its land.
- Line blocking, forage against pillage, the levy and the AI's post read the claim where
  they read the ring today.
- Festivals stick both ways: a clan keeps its festival until it's clearly not worth the
  walk, and keeps a new one some turns. Spacing and reach come down so festivals are nearer
  and more.
- Trails by traffic: every walk adds its people to every tile it crosses, home included; a
  tile wears a trail above a threshold and loses it below. A camp's line and a scout's route
  count as walked while they're used.
- Desert costs more to cross than plains.
- The page draws each civ's land as a border, tints leaning clans by civ, drops the capital's
  circles and the market ring, and shows who walks to a selected festival.
- The judge gains Standing on land, land changing hands, clans leaving, and runs the claim
  against a control with no claims.

**The designer settles:** the guard's floor and labour; how fast leaning grows by land; the
leaving run; the festival margins, spacing and reach; the trail threshold and fade; how the
AI claims until R (a ring of its clans' homes is enough).

**In scope:** the claim, the guard, leaning and leaving by land, the levy by land, the
festival and trail rules, desert, the page's border and tints, the judge's checks, the
sweep's control, tests, the design edits.

**Out of scope:** fighting over a contested tile (A); the AI's claiming beyond a stand-in
(R); goods.

**After:** #H, #G

### P. The page becomes the game

**Goal:** The page is a game you can start, play and come back to, laid out as `page.md`
says: a start screen with new game, continue and load; the play screen with the map, a top
bar that shows every civ's Standing, a bottom panel in one shape for whatever is selected
with one button row, and the feed and alerts on the right; space ends the turn; a "Move
to…" button; debug views under a Debug menu; autosave to the browser each turn; and an end
screen that the engine can fill once a game can end (R). Under it, the page's code takes
the shape it grows in: screens as modules, a view model built once a turn, a draw layer per
mechanic, one panel component, one atlas for placeholder art, and one play driver. Every
touch point has a play script, and the PR carries screenshots at three window sizes.

**Spec:** opus medium: the whole page, judged by eye

**Spec-review:** opus medium

**Shape:** Easel and Canvas: the structure, the screens, the panel, the keys, autosave and
the atlas are the easel, settled by `page.md`. The canvas is the layout and feel: it plays
every touch point, reads its own screenshots, and fixes what reads badly.

**Design:** docs/design/page.md, docs/design/map.md, docs/design/founding.md

**Decided:**
- Screens: start, pick, play, end, each a module; `main.js` only routes between them.
- A view model built from `Sim.view()` once a turn, and every layer and panel reads it.
  `drawCivs` splits into layers: land, held ground, lines, units, routes, trails, leanings.
- One panel component at the bottom, fed a selection: a name and a line of what it's doing,
  its numbers, its latest reasons, and the button row `page.md` gives each kind. The camp's
  and scout's panels become that component; "Move to…" arms a click as "Route to here" does.
- The top bar: turn, Standing per civ with the led one marked, End turn, Play and speed,
  Game and View menus; debug views move under Debug.
- Keys as `page.md` says. The legend and tooltips stay; every number in the bar and the
  panel has a tooltip.
- Autosave to `localStorage` after every turn, guarded for a browser that refuses it;
  Continue on the start screen; Save and Load as files stay.
- One atlas file and a loader; tiles and units drawn from it with placeholder cells drawn
  by the build, so paid art replaces a file. `www/assets/atlas.json` names every cell and
  its size, which is the brief an artist gets. A sound bus fed by feed events, with
  `www/assets/sounds.json` mapping event kinds to files, silent until a file exists.
- `play.mjs` is the one driver; the per-piece scripts become one script per touch point
  that exits non-zero when the touch point is missing. CI runs them. Screenshots at phone,
  laptop and wide sizes go in `relay/specs/<n>/screens/` and the PR description.
- Nothing in the engine changes except what the view needs.

**The designer settles:** the panel's exact rows per kind; the atlas's cell layout; how
the end screen reads before R fills it; whether the feed stays beside the map or over it at
phone width.

**In scope:** the page's structure, the screens, the panel, the bar, keys, autosave, the
atlas, the play scripts, the screenshots, `engine/CLAUDE.md`'s `www` row.

**Out of scope:** new rules; sound; first-time hints (M10); paid art.

**After:** #L

### A. Armies

**Goal:** A civ raises as many armies as it can feed, of kinds, and fights for ground with
them the way `war.md` tells it: guards skirmish along a contested border with small losses
to both sides; a big stack takes thinly held tiles and is slowed by a guarded capital;
defenders entrench; an army cut off starves and surrenders; a capital falls when its militia
is gone. The scout becomes a unit like the others and stops starving on its first errand.
The question: played as scripts on the retro maps, does the Red and Blue story come out,
and does fighting move Standing?

**Spec:** opus medium: the fight rule and unit kinds are the hard centre of the milestone

**Spec-review:** opus medium

**Shape:** Clay, Kiln, Glaze: the clay is the fight rule and the unit kinds on the units
module, with its invariants and tests. The kilns are raising kinds, militia, siege and
surrender, the scout as a unit, and the page. The glaze tunes losses, entrenchment, retreat
and surrender together until the story plays out on real maps.

**Design:** docs/design/war.md, docs/design/supply.md, docs/design/founding.md,
docs/design/scouting.md, docs/design/actors.md

**Decided:**
- A civ may have any number of armies. An army is a `Unit` with a stack: people by kind.
  Raise names a kind and a place (the capital, or an army to join); disband names an army.
  Each army has its own pack, line, switches and zone, as the camp has.
- Kinds in this piece: **warriors** and **archers**. Archers are raised only with a turn of
  bow-making labour on forest the civ holds; nothing else is spent, and there is no wood
  good yet. Each kind has an attack and a defence strength in `data.rs`. Horsemen wait for
  herds.
- **Fighting** happens only on a contested tile: one where people of two civs stand, or a
  claimed tile a rival enters. Each turn each side there loses a share of its people by the
  other side's strength there; the stronger side holds the tile. A defender that stayed put
  entrenches, up to a cap; a side losing badly retreats to its nearest held tile. One rule,
  in one module, with the contest in `hold` folded into it.
- **Militia:** a clan's people defend their home tile at a fraction of a warrior's strength
  when a rival pillages or enters it. The capital's militia is its clan plus the guard drawn
  back to it.
- **Surrender:** an army hungry past a threshold surrenders: its people join the nearest clan
  of the civ holding the ground, or disband home if none holds it. Both civs hear.
- **A capital falls** when a rival army stands on it with no militia left. The civ is out:
  its land and clans go unaligned, its armies disband; the game's end is R's.
- The **scout** is a kind of unit with the same switches, forages the tiles round it, takes
  only what it needs, refills its pack, keeps its route's trail while the route is used, and
  can be raised again when lost. The camp's foraging strips only what it takes.
- The page: armies drawn as stacks with their size; the unit panel's row gains Raise by kind;
  skirmish, retreat, surrender and a fallen capital in the feed and alerts; "Why" explains a
  fight's outcome.
- Three play scripts on the retro maps make the story: a border skirmish, a stack against a
  guarded capital, a surrounded army surrendering. The judge gains people lost to fighting,
  tiles changing hands by fighting, and armies surrendered, against a control with no
  fighting.

**The designer settles:** the loss rate and its curve; entrenchment's rate and cap; the
retreat threshold; the surrender threshold; the militia fraction; what a fallen capital's
store does; whether archers' strength differs in attack and defence or in range; the
scout's foraging reach.

**In scope:** stacks and kinds, raising and disbanding by kind, the fight rule, militia,
surrender, the fallen capital, the scout as a unit, the camp's foraging, the page, the
scripts, the judge's checks, tests, the design edits.

**Out of scope:** the AI fighting (R); horsemen; walls, forts and sieges of cities (M9);
knacks (M5); goods.

**After:** #P

### R. A rival that plays, and a game that ends

**Goal:** The AI plays for Standing with every lever the player has, and a game ends: lost
when your capital falls, won when yours is the last standing or the highest Standing at the
turn limit. The AI claims and guards land, raises what it can feed, gifts where clans are
contested, pillages a rival's clans, cuts a line it can reach, answers a cut, marches on a
capital it can take and pulls back from one it can't. The end screen shows how it went. The
question: is a led game ever in doubt? On the retro maps the led civ's rank changes after
the pick, some games end before the limit, and G's player loses some.

**Spec:** opus medium: the AI is judgement work, and the end rules decide what the game is

**Spec-review:** opus medium

**Shape:** Easel and Canvas: the end rules, the turn limit, the end screen, Standing over
time and an AI planner that gives orders through `World::order` are the easel. The canvas
tunes the planner's priorities until games are in doubt and end, measured by the judge and
by G's player.

**Design:** docs/design/war.md, docs/design/vision.md, docs/design/founding.md

**Decided:**
- A game ends when one capital stands, or at a turn limit per map size. The winner is the
  last capital, else the highest Standing. `World.phase` gains `Over`, with the winner and
  why; orders after it are refused; the page shows the end screen and lets the game run on.
- Standing per civ per turn is kept for the end screen's lines and the judge.
- The AI planner runs once per civ per turn through `World::order`: it reads a few needs in
  priority order (feed the armies, hold the land, grow Standing, hurt the nearest rival,
  take a capital it outweighs) and gives the orders that serve the first unmet one. Its
  orders are the player's orders; it has no others.
- The AI claims a connected patch round its clans' homes and widens it while its guard
  holds; raises armies only while its store and lines feed them; gifts at festivals where a
  rival also gifts; pillages a rival's clans it can reach; paints a zone across a rival line
  it can reach and keeps it while it feeds; answers a cut by marching home or breaking out;
  marches on a capital whose militia it outweighs and withdraws when hungry.
- The page's end screen: won or lost, how, each civ's Standing over the game, play on or new
  game. The pick's cards show the turn limit.
- The judge gains games ended before the limit, turns to the end, the led civ's rank changes,
  and lead changes; the bands require the lead to change on most maps.
- G's player plays three seeds and the judge reads them; the play notes go in
  `relay/specs/<n>/play.md`.

**The designer settles:** the turn limit per size; the planner's priorities and thresholds;
how the AI decides it outweighs a capital; what "in doubt" measures on a single map.

**In scope:** the end rules, the phase, Standing over time, the end screen, the planner, the
judge's checks, the play notes, tests, the design edits (`vision.md` Winning included).

**Out of scope:** difficulty settings; more than three civs; alliances; any new unit or good.

**After:** #A

## 4. What you play at the end

A game on the page, from the start screen, against two rivals, with a goal. You claim land
and see it thin as you widen it. Clans on it come to you, and leave when a rival feeds them
better. You raise warriors and archers as far as your store and lines feed them, paint a
border, and skirmish along it. You march a stack at a capital and find out whether its
militia and your hunger stop you. You cut a rival army off and watch it surrender, or lose
yours that way. The game ends, and tells you why. Under it, every PR has been judged on
Standing and doubt, and a session has played it and said what it couldn't tell.

Then the retro, and M4: settling.
