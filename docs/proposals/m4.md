# M4: Settling

What the M3 retro ([m3-retro.md](m3-retro.md)) leads to. M3 built every system but R and left a
game with no finish line and no store that can feed an army. M4 gives the game an end, an
economy that settles and fills a store worth taking, the day as the yardstick everything is
priced in, and then the rival that plays for it. Under it, the game proves itself: a harness
that places units on a known map and asserts what they do, and a judge that compares a change
against main with a noise floor.

The Relay and process changes that go with it are in [m4-relay.md](m4-relay.md). The visions
the pieces build to are `docs/design/labour.md` and `farming.md`, drafted from your notes for
you to correct, with `war.md` and `page.md` as they stand. The scores are in
[`docs/process.md`](../process.md).

## Your six answers

The retro asked six questions. These are the answers the pieces below assume; change any and
the pieces change with it.

1. **The turn stays a year, and M4 doesn't rescale distance.** Every number is labelled in
   days, walks are charged per person carrying, and the supply ring is computed and drawn. Which
   clock war runs on is decided before M5.
2. **#105 is closed and re-cut**: the end rules are piece E, the planner is piece R.
3. **PR 124 is closed and remade** in the Relay change: the player and the trial's record carried
   over, the bands dropped, the judge prompt rewritten as the review's rubric.
4. **A trail on flat land makes a difference**, starting at three quarters of the bare cost,
   with the pre-game sweep re-run. D then makes a trail on flat land the unit step: one day.
5. **The pack is the train**, not what one man carries, until animals exist (M5).
6. **Relay gains the play step, the fix-or-ask rule with a fixes piece, and the milestone-built
   rule now**; the retro run is a try on five angles in four sessions.

## 1. Foundation

Two pieces that run first, in either order. Neither changes a rule a player meets except E's
end and start.

### S. The game proves itself

**Goal:** Anything in the game can be placed on a known map, given orders and checked, with the
real engine; and a change can be compared against main with the drift measured, so a review
knows a move from noise. The question is health and decisions: the two bugs you found playing
are tests that fail on main and pass after T, and every lever the player has is pressed once by
a script that checks its effect on the world.

**Spec:** sonnet high: settled work, and the probe for a cheaper designer on a Henry Ford piece

**Spec-review:** opus medium

**Play:** none

**Shape:** Henry Ford: the harness, the compare and the economy group are settled in their
rules; the digests are the proof.

**Design:** docs/architecture.md, docs/process.md

**Decided:**

- A `Stage` in core (`core/src/stage.rs`), public like `script.rs`: a map from the `map`
  command's own text (`map::parse` round-trips `map::ascii` on the retro maps) or a retro map
  at the pick from a per-process cache; layers for hidden costs and marks; clans, civs and
  armies placed; orders through `World::order` or `act`; `turn`, `until`; reads for revealed,
  trail, route cost, people, store, holder and events. A staged world is not a save: it asserts
  outcomes.
- `tests/common` and `core/tests/scout.rs`'s painters are rewritten on it. The first two cases
  are the scout that must check every mountain that could open its route (ending in the
  invariant that when a scout settles nothing unrevealed could beat its route) and Disband all
  sending every person home at once. Both fail on main.
- `Sim.stage` in wasm and a `check.mjs` arm, so the page's panel shapes are tested under Node as
  data: the Disband all case has a page arm, because the bug is in the page's order form.
- `sansfiat judge --compare A.json B.json`: every metric of two `sweep --judge --json` runs side
  by side, the change, and a noise floor per metric from a null perturbation (the sweep once
  more with one harmless difference at the pick); a change is flagged only past its floor.
- `judge FILE` gains an idle led civ as a second control: the same map with the led civ given no
  order.
- `sweep --judge` gains an economy group: store mean and max, turns at a pack's worth, levy a
  turn, the largest army, the guard's people.
- `bench --after N` times the turns past the pick; the Scale row in `architecture.md` names it.
- `www/play/buttons.mjs` presses every button of every panel once on a staged game and checks
  the world changed as the button says; `check.sh` runs it.
- `cli/src/judge.rs` splits into the sweep's judge and the file's judge.

**In scope:** the stage, the parser, the rewritten fixtures, the two cases, the page arm, the
compare, the floor, the idle control, the economy group, `bench --after`, the buttons script,
the split, `engine/CLAUDE.md`.

**Out of scope:** fixing what the two cases find (T); any rule; the judge's rubric (the Relay
change).

### E. A game that ends

**Goal:** A game ends: lost when your capital falls, won when yours is the last standing or the
highest Standing at the turn limit, and the end screen says how it went. The question is doubt:
some games end before the limit on the stories' maps, and every game ends; and the judge reads
the end on every later piece.

**Spec:** opus medium: the end rules decide what the game is

**Spec-review:** opus medium

**Play:** sonnet

**Shape:** Easel and Canvas: the phase, the limit, Standing over time and the end screen are the
easel; the canvas sets the turn limit per size against the retro maps and the stories so a game
that ends by conquest can, and one that doesn't ends in time to matter.

**Design:** docs/design/vision.md, docs/design/war.md, docs/design/founding.md,
docs/design/page.md

**Decided:**

- `World.phase` gains `Over`, with the winner and why. A game ends when one capital stands, or at
  a turn limit per map size. The winner is the last capital, else the highest Standing. Orders
  after it are refused; the page lets the game run on.
- Standing per civ per turn is kept for the end screen's lines and the judge.
- The end screen: won or lost, how, each civ's Standing over the game, play on or new game. The
  pick's cards show the turn limit. "The game goes on" goes.
- The judge gains games ended before the limit, turns to the end and the led civ's rank at the
  end, against the no-fight control. `judge FILE` reports the end.
- `vision.md` Winning: the first way to win is written into the prose.
- A civ starts with its scout and nothing else: `ARMY.start` and `GUARD.start` are 0, and the
  boon year's food stays in the store. The AI's stand-in raises its guard as it does now; it
  raises no army until R. The stories raise what they script.

**The designer settles:** the turn limit per size; what the end screen shows for a watched
game; whether a fallen civ's player may keep watching.

**In scope:** the phase, the limit, Standing over time, the end screen, the start, the judge's
checks, the play scripts, the stories, the design edits.

**Out of scope:** the AI playing for the end (R); difficulty; more than three civs.

## 2. The game

Five pieces in this order: T, D, F, W, R. Each is a system the player meets, cut into builds by
area, with one question in the scores.

### T. Scouts that work

**Goal:** A scout does what its buttons say and lives through its errand, and a trail makes a
difference wherever it is. The question is decisions and health: the scout's three buttons each
have a visible result, the scout lives through its first errand on every retro map (13 of 14
die today), and a trail on flat land moves trade and Standing on the retro maps against main
without stilling the map.

**Spec:** opus medium: a rule the player meets, and a tuning the pre-game depends on

**Spec-review:** opus medium

**Play:** sonnet

**Shape:** Easel and Canvas: the buttons, the search's order and reasons, Disband all and the
scout's feeding are the easel, proved by S's stage; the canvas is the flat trail, tuned against
the pre-game sweep and `sweep --judge`.

**Design:** docs/design/scouting.md, docs/design/supply.md, docs/design/page.md,
docs/design/map.md

**Decided:**

- The scout's row: Move to…, Route from…, Route to…. Move to… is a new scout order with a
  destination: it walks there and stays, foraging. Route to… is today's "Move to…" re    named.
- Disband all is a button that sends no count, for every unit kind.
- The search orders a range's mountains by their best case (the cost through them plus the walk
  there), not by nearness. The panel's Unrevealed row says why a mountain is left: it can't beat
  the known route, with the two costs.
- A scout forages only what a tile regrows: it never strips a tile, and no tile it forages lies
  fallow. Armies forage as now.
- A hungry scout wanders: it leaves its errand for the nearest tile in range with food to spare,
  forages there until fed, and goes back to its errand. An idle scout does the same, so it
  waits on rich ground rather than at the bare capital.
- Flat land has a hidden trail cost like rough land: a trail on plains or coast brings the tile
  to three quarters of its bare cost, worn by traffic as now. `scouting.md`'s "flat land gains
  nothing from a trail" is reworded.
- The scout's hunger has a reason, and Move to… and Route to… warn past the line's reach.
- `scout_errand` becomes a test on the stage: every scout alive at 50 turns.

**The designer settles:** how far a hungry scout wanders and when it counts as fed; whether
it also needs one person or a bigger pack to live through its errand; the flat trail's value against the pre-game
ending by centres and the AI's orders not falling by half; whether the scout builds flat trails
or only wears them.

**In scope:** the orders, the buttons, the search, the panel, the feeding, the flat trail, the
tests, the design edits.

**Out of scope:** the supply ring (D); pack animals; fog.

**After:** #S

### D. The day

**Goal:** Every number in the game reads in days of one person's labour and turns of one person's
eating; an actor splits its days between jobs before each job divides over its tiles; and the
supply ring is drawn: the tiles from which an army's line feeds it with days to spare. The
question is motion and health: no digest moves under the clay; the ring is where armies and
scouts live and past it where they starve, on the retro maps; and a walk costs more for a clan
that carries more.

**Spec:** opus medium: the clay every M4 rule stands on

**Spec-review:** opus medium

**Play:** sonnet

**Shape:** Clay and Kiln: the clay is `actors::split` with jobs and a shared labour ladder for
the units, digest-safe; the kilns are the labels, the per-person walk, the ring in core and on
the page, and the judge's measure.

**Design:** docs/design/labour.md, docs/design/actors.md, docs/design/supply.md

**Decided:**

- `actors::split(labour, routes, shares, jobs)`: the routes are paid once, what's left goes to
  each job by its share, and each job divides over its tiles as `divide` does. With one job it
  is `divide` exactly, and `reap` calls it; `stats::digest` is unchanged for every seed.
- The camp's and the scout's turns spend `left` down one `Ladder` type, the same subtractions in
  the same order. The guard eats through the actor, from the civ's store and then its clans'.
- Every number in `data.rs` is labelled with its unit: days a person a turn, food a person a
  turn, days a tile. `view.rs` and the page's tooltips show days. No arithmetic changes.
- A step is priced so a trail on flat land costs one day: every terrain and hidden trail cost
  is scaled by 4/3 (bare plains 1.33, forest and hills 2.67, marsh and a pass 4), `MAP`'s
  hidden-cost rolls become `f64` as the tiles already are, and every budget spent in step cost
  scales with them, so no reach changes. `sweep --judge --compare` shows no move past the floor.
- A walk is charged per person walking, not once per actor: a clan of twenty carrying its
  surplus pays more than a clan of two. A rule change; `SAVE_VERSION` bumps.
- `units::line::ring(civ)`: for every tile a line may reach, the share of an army's days the line
  takes to feed it; inside the ring that share is under one. `Sim.ring`; a `ring.js` layer drawn
  when an army or the civ is selected; the judge counts tiles in the ring, held tiles in it, and
  army-turns spent outside it.
- The pack is the train: `supply.md` says so, and its size stays in turns.
- `units/mod.rs` splits: raising and disbanding into `units/muster.rs`, `strip` and `supply`
  beside the line.

**The designer settles:** how the per-person walk is weighted so festivals and territories don't
collapse; what the ring's layer shows at a glance.

**In scope:** the split, the ladder, the guard's meal, the labels, the step's unit, the per-person walk, the ring
in core, wasm, the page and the judge, the split of `units/mod.rs`, tests, the design edits.

**Out of scope:** rescaling a tile to a day; sub-steps inside a turn; the farmer's job (F).

**After:** #T

### F. Fields

**Goal:** The Neolithic revolution begins: a clan puts some of its days to fields as its grain
tames, and a settled civ feeds more people on less land and fills a store worth taking. The
question is Standing and motion: on the retro maps a civ whose clans farm gains Standing over
one whose clans don't, its store holds an army's packs on most maps (14 of 21 never do today),
and farming clans stop moving.

**Spec:** opus medium: a new mechanic the player meets, and the economy the rest of M4 and R
stand on

**Spec-review:** opus medium

**Play:** sonnet

**Shape:** Clay, Kiln, Glaze: the clay is the field job on the clan with its split moved a notch
a turn; the kilns are fields on river-side ground, the home following the fields, the
levy from farmers, the page and the feed; the glaze tunes yield and the split's step
together until a settled civ pays and nobody starves who shouldn't.

**Design:** docs/design/farming.md, docs/design/clans.md, docs/design/actors.md,
docs/design/founding.md, docs/design/page.md

**Decided:**

- A clan has two jobs: gathering over its territory, and fields. The split is state on the clan
  and moves one notch a turn toward the split that feeds it better, as `change` moves one tile.
  Nobody orders a clan to farm.
- A field is days on a tile's field ground: the river-side and floodplain share of a tile, which
  the map rolls. A field yields grain per day in proportion to the tile's fertility and its
  tame, far more per day than gathering wild grain, and it doesn't wear.
- No seed: a field's yield is net of it. A starving farmer's split falls back a notch a turn,
  as it rose.
- A farmer's home follows its fields, not the middle of its territory.
- The levy, the store and gifts work as now; a farming clan's bigger surplus fills the store.
- The civ panel shows how its people live: gathering, farming, in the army. The clan panel shows
  a farmer's fields. Fields are drawn on the map. The feed tells when a clan takes up
  fields.
- The judge gains fields, farming people, and store turns at a pack's worth; the sweep runs a
  no-fields control.

**The designer settles:** the field ground per terrain and river; the yield curve against tame;
the split's notch and its margin; how tame moves under fields.

**In scope:** the field job, field ground, the home rule, the page, the feed, the judge's
checks, the control, tests, the design edits.

**Out of scope:** towns (W); irrigation; animals; crops but grain; good and bad years.

**After:** #D

### W. Towns

**Goal:** Many clans live on one tile, fields along the water and homes at the market, and the
engine carries it. The question is motion and health: towns form on the retro maps where
farmers and a festival meet, a town's clans trade more and walk less, and a large map runs
under a bound past the pick.

**Spec:** opus medium: a rule change at the engine's centre with a scale target

**Spec-review:** opus medium

**Play:** sonnet

**Shape:** Easel and Canvas: homes that don't claim, shared field ground, the festival search
once per home tile and clans indexed by market are the easel, each digest-safe where it can be;
the canvas is whether towns form and feed, and the turn's cost on a large map.

**Design:** docs/design/farming.md, docs/design/clans.md, docs/architecture.md

**Decided:**

- A home is not a claim: a home tile can hold many homes. Clans' gathering territories stay
  exclusive.
- A tile's field ground is shared by area among the clans whose fields are on it, bounded by the
  ground the tile allows.
- A town is what a tile looks like with several homes and a festival; it has no rule of its own.
  The page draws it as many hearths on one tile; the feed tells when a town forms.
- The festival search runs once per home tile per turn; clans are indexed by market once a turn;
  the AI's rival paths are cached per turn.
- `bench --after` on the large retro map is the scale check, with its bound in `judge.toml`'s
  place: `architecture.md`'s Scale row.

**The designer settles:** when a tile reads as a town; the scale bound; whether a staged change
keeps every digest until the last step.

**In scope:** homes, field ground, the search, the index, the cache, the page, the judge, the
bench, tests, the design edits.

**Out of scope:** walls, cities as actors, irrigation (M10); the town's store as a thing.

**After:** #F

### R. A rival that plays

**Goal:** The AI plays for Standing with every lever the player has, on an economy that can feed
an army: it settles where it can, claims and guards, raises what it can feed, gifts where clans
are contested, pillages a rival's clans, cuts a line it can reach, answers a cut, marches on a
capital it can take and pulls back from one it can't. The question is doubt: is a led game ever
in doubt? On the retro maps the led civ's rank changes after the pick, some games end before the
limit, a led player beats an idle led civ by more than the floor, and the play step's player
loses some.

**Spec:** opus medium: the AI is judgement work

**Spec-review:** opus medium

**Play:** sonnet, three seeds

**Shape:** Easel and Canvas: a planner that gives orders through `World::order` is the easel;
the canvas tunes its priorities against the judge, the idle control and the player until games
are in doubt and end.

**Design:** docs/design/war.md, docs/design/vision.md, docs/design/founding.md,
docs/design/farming.md

**Decided:**

- The planner runs once per civ per turn through `World::order`: it reads a few needs in
  priority order (feed the armies, hold the land, grow Standing, hurt the nearest rival, take a
  capital it outweighs) and gives the orders that serve the first unmet one. Its orders are the
  player's orders; it has no others. The stand-in claim goes.
- It claims a connected patch round its clans' homes and widens it while its guard holds; raises
  armies only while its store and lines feed them, reading the ring; gifts at festivals where a
  rival also gifts; pillages a rival's clans it can reach; paints a zone across a rival line and
  keeps it while it feeds; answers a cut by marching home or breaking out; marches on a capital
  whose militia it outweighs and withdraws when hungry.
- Clans leaving is tuned so Standing is losable: the run or the margin moves until clans leave on
  the retro maps.
- The judge gains the led civ's rank changes and lead changes against the idle control; the play
  step plays three seeds.

**The designer settles:** the planner's priorities and thresholds; how it decides it outweighs
a capital; what "in doubt" measures on one map.

**In scope:** the planner, leaving's tuning, the judge's checks, the play notes, tests, the
design edits.

**Out of scope:** difficulty; alliances; any new unit or good.

**After:** #W

## 4. What you play at the end

A game from the start screen that ends and says why. Your clans along the river take up fields
and stop moving; a town forms at your capital; the store fills for the first time. You raise an
army that can march, and the ring on the map shows how far it can go before every day goes on
staying fed. A rival settles too, comes for your store, and takes it if your guard is thin. You
cut it off and watch it surrender, or lose yours that way. Under it, every PR has been played by
a session before its review, judged against main with the drift measured, and the retro has
started without you.

Then the retro, and M5: herds and beer.
