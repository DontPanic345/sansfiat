# M5: Holding and herding

What the M4 retro ([m4-retro.md](m4-retro.md)) leads to. M4 built the economy and the rival,
and left a game decided at the pick: nothing ends before its limit, nobody loses land, and a
player on a small map ends turns. M5 first makes a small map a game, from your notes: land
that armies hold and take, scouts that finish and can die, a page you can play and watch
rules on, a town where you look. Then the clay the code needs, herds, and pack animals.

The Relay changes that go with it are in [m5-relay.md](m5-relay.md), and are built in this PR.
Every piece runs whole on Fable: that is M5's experiment. The scores are in
[`docs/process.md`](../process.md).

## Your five answers

The retro asked five questions. These are the answers the pieces assume; change one and the
pieces change with it.

1. **Land is held by being claimed, and lost by being walked on.** A claimed tile joined to
   the capital is the civ's land until a rival army ends its turn on it. The guard and the
   militia go. A capital falls when a rival army stands on it with no army of its own there.
   Changes L, and what C's zone means.
2. **M5 is your notes, then herds and pack animals.** Beer moves to the head of M6. Changes
   which pieces are here.
3. **The clock is closed as built.** The turn stays a year, distance isn't rescaled, and an
   animal is priced in the days it saves a walk. Changes H and K's numbers.
4. **A civ's capital counts its festival for a town.** The town rule stays: three clans on a
   tile with a festival. Changes N.
5. **No looser `fix` rule.** A designer takes the obvious ideas into its piece instead.

## 1. A small map is a game

Four pieces from your notes, in order.

### L. Land without a guard

**Goal:** A civ's land is its claim, held with nobody standing on it and lost to a rival army
that walks onto it, so land and capitals change hands. The guard and the militia are gone from
the rules, the planner, the page and the text view. The question is doubt: with every civ on
the AI, do games end before the limit and do capitals fall? On main 0 of 7 end early, 0
capitals fall and 14 tiles are taken; the control is main's sweep.

**Whole:** fable medium

**Play:** sonnet

**Shape:** Clay and Kiln: what holds land is unsettled until it runs on the retro maps, and
the planner, page and view follow it.

**Design:** docs/design/war.md, docs/design/founding.md, docs/design/vision.md,
docs/design/page.md

**Decided:**

- A claimed tile joined to the capital is the civ's land. A rival army that ends its turn on
  it takes it, and with it any part of the claim no longer joined to the capital.
- No guard and no militia: neither is raised, shown, fed or counted. Clans don't defend their
  homes.
- A capital falls when a rival army stands on it and no army of its civ does.
- Only armies fight. The planner holds land with armies and reads what an army costs to feed.
- A fight among three civs on one tile is a rule, not a panic (#291).
- The view says what a claim order does to the claim and names its tiles.

**In scope:** `claim.rs`, the fight, the planner, the page's guard row and the text view's
guard lines, the judge's guard figures, tests on the stage, the ledger lines the guard had.

**Out of scope:** new unit kinds; walls; any change to how armies are fed.

### C. Scouts that finish, and can die

**Goal:** A scout eats at its capital: when its pack runs low it walks home, fills it from the
store and walks back to its errand, and it doesn't wait on rich ground. An army that meets a
rival's scout on its tile kills it, and a scout can't enter or leave a tile in a rival army's
zone. The question is decisions: does a led scout's errand finish? On small 61288 the route
from Red's capital to Gold's stands unfinished from +10 to +200
(`relay/retro/m4/game/scout-61288.txt`); the control is that game on main.

**Whole:** fable medium

**Play:** sonnet

**Shape:** Easel and Canvas: the rules are settled; how far a scout gets on a pack, and when
it turns for home, is tuned on the retro maps.

**Design:** docs/design/scouting.md, docs/design/supply.md, docs/design/war.md

**Decided:**

- A scout's food is its pack, filled at its capital. Foraging on an errand goes.
- A scout's line in the view and its panel say what it is doing this turn: searching, walking
  home to eat, walking back, or trapped.
- A rival army on a scout's tile kills the scout. A scout in a rival's zone stays where it is
  until the zone lifts or the army leaves.
- The scout's test is a built map on the stage: a known route, a pack that needs one trip
  home, and the errand done by a named turn.

**In scope:** `scout.rs` and its search, the scout's supply, the fight's scout rule, the zone,
the panel and view lines, stage tests.

**Out of scope:** AI orders for scouts; new scout orders.

**After:** #L

### P. The page, played

**Goal:** The page is played by a session as a player plays it, in a browser, on small 61288
and one retro map, and its three worst faults are fixed. A click centres the camera on the
clicked tile, so cycling a tile's units doesn't move the view. The gift line says how many
clans the gift reaches and what was left unspent. The question is decisions: orders a player
gives per hundred turns, and turns with none, in the played games; the control is the check's
game on main (11 orders in 103 turns, 95 turns with none).

**Whole:** fable medium

**Play:** sonnet

**Shape:** Magnum: what is worst is known only by playing, and each fix is small.

**Design:** docs/design/page.md, docs/design/founding.md

**Decided:**

- A click on a tile centres the camera on that tile's middle.
- The gift's readout, in the panel and the text view: clans it reaches, food given, food left.
- The session writes what it found playing the page, worst first, in its spec folder, fixes
  the top three that need no rule change, and files the rest as ideas.
- When nothing a player can order would change Standing, the view says so and names what
  would bring a clan or a rival in reach.

**In scope:** the page, the text view, page scripts on the stage.

**Out of scope:** new rules; art; sound.

**After:** #C

### V. Tests you can watch

**Goal:** Every test on the stage has a name, a map and a claim, and the page lists them: you
pick one, watch it play on its small map, and see it pass or fail, with the same code the
test runs. The scout's test from C is the first. The page scripts that read a seeded pick
move to the stage. The question is health: page scripts and core tests that read a retro
pick, 16 of 19 and 10 of 39 on main; the control is main's count.

**Whole:** fable medium

**Play:** none

**Shape:** Henry Ford: the stage exists, and each test moved is the same work.

**Design:** docs/design/page.md, docs/architecture.md

**Decided:**

- A stage test is data the engine and the page both read: the map as text, the units, the
  orders, the turns and what must hold.
- The page has a Tests screen: the list, a map view of the chosen test, Play, and the result.
- `cargo test` runs the same list.
- A page script reads a retro pick only where its point is the retro map.

**In scope:** `stage.rs`, the wasm page's stage door, the Tests screen, the page scripts.

**Out of scope:** new rules; the retro maps' bound tests in core.

**After:** #P

## 2. The clay

### Y. Goods, carrying and data

**Goal:** The code is ready for a good that isn't eaten and an animal that carries: a goods
table with each good's weight, spoiling and use; one carrying rule on the actor that the
festival walk, the pack and the line read; `data.rs` as a folder; the planner's paths cached.
The sweep reports numbers and gives no verdicts. The question is health: every digest is the
one main gives, and a turn on large 8 costs less than main's 22.9 ms; the control is main.

**Whole:** fable medium

**Play:** none

**Shape:** Henry Ford: each part is settled and the digests are the proof.

**Design:** docs/architecture.md, docs/design/actors.md, docs/design/supply.md

**Decided:**

- A `Good` row carries weight, spoil, whether it is eaten, and its use. Eating, spoiling,
  `total` and the market's worth read it. No good is added.
- One `carry` on the actor.
- `data/` has a file per system, re-exported as `data::*`; CLAUDE.md's "every number lives in
  `data`" means the folder.
- The planner's capital-to-capital paths are cached a turn, and reach is a flood fill.
- `sweep --judge` prints no `pass` or `MISS`. A piece's question names its number and control.
- `engine/CLAUDE.md`'s layout is a line a folder.

**In scope:** core, the CLI's judge, `engine/CLAUDE.md`, `docs/architecture.md`.

**Out of scope:** any rule a player meets; any number.

**After:** #V

## 3. The game grows

### N. A town where you look

**Goal:** A civ's capital becomes a town on a small map: its festival counts, and clans that
farm near it come to live there. The question is motion: towns at +200 on the seven retro
maps, 13 on 4 maps on main with none on small 3, small 9 or medium 8, and trade per person in
a town against outside it; the control is main's sweep.

**Whole:** fable medium

**Play:** sonnet

**Shape:** Easel and Canvas: the rule is one line, and where towns then form is read on the
maps.

**Design:** docs/design/farming.md, docs/design/clans.md

**Decided:**

- A capital's market is a festival for the town rule.
- A town is still three clans on one tile with a festival.
- The page marks the capital's town as any other.

**In scope:** homes, the town rule, the judge's towns group, the page's town mark.

**Out of scope:** cities; walls; anything a town does beyond trade.

**After:** #Y

### H. Herds

**Goal:** A herd is a store that walks and breeds: clans on grass keep animals, which graze
their land, breed by what the land carries, and are eaten or traded. Pastoral clans hold wide,
thin land. The question is Standing: do civs on grass gain against the no-herds control, as
farming civs gained 5.2 points against no fields?

**Whole:** fable medium

**Play:** sonnet

**Shape:** Clay, Kiln and Glaze: the herd on the actor is the clay, grazing and pastoral land
the kiln, and the numbers only work together.

**Design:** docs/design/animals.md, docs/design/clans.md, docs/design/actors.md

**Decided:**

- A herd is a state on the actor: animals in its store that breed by the tile rule and graze
  the clan's territory through the labour split.
- Animals are a good with a use: eaten, and traded at festivals.
- A herding clan moves with its herd; a farmer doesn't herd.
- The page draws herds and the clan panel counts them.

**In scope:** the herd, grazing, pastoral territory, the judge's herds group with a no-herds
control, the page.

**Out of scope:** pack animals; horsemen; milk, wool and hides.

**After:** #N

### K. Pack animals and horsemen

**Goal:** Animals carry: a pack animal on a line cuts what the line costs and carries it
further, so the ring widens, and a civ with herds can raise horsemen, who march further in a
turn. The question is decisions and doubt: the ring's tiles for a civ with pack animals
against one without, and tiles taken and games ended before the limit; the control is the
sweep with animals carrying nothing.

**Whole:** fable medium

**Play:** sonnet

**Shape:** Easel and Canvas: carrying is one rule after Y; what a horseman is worth is tuned
against the judge.

**Design:** docs/design/animals.md, docs/design/supply.md, docs/design/war.md,
docs/design/labour.md

**Decided:**

- A pack animal changes `carry`, and with it the festival walk, the pack and the line.
- Horsemen are a kind raised only by a civ with herds; they march further and eat more.
- An animal is priced in days: what it carries and the days it saves a walk.
- The planner uses both.

**In scope:** carrying, the ring, the horseman kind, the planner, the page.

**Out of scope:** the camel; chariots; cavalry tactics.

**After:** #H

## 4. Order and cost

L, C, P, V, Y, N, H, K, one at a time: each `After` holds a piece until the one before has
merged. A whole piece starts only on a fresh 5-hour window.

The only measure of a whole piece on Fable is R: $28 to $32 at list, 140 to 160 5-hour points
and two windows, 1.3 times its stepped plan. Eight such pieces are about $240 and sixteen
windows. M4's eight pieces took 68 weekly points; at 1.3 times that, M5 is about 90, so two
weeks at the weekly ramp. P and V are smaller than R, and H is larger. The retro checks all
of this: what a window buys is the milestone's question about Relay.

## 5. What you play at the end

A small map where your land is yours until someone walks onto it. Your scout goes out, comes
home to eat, and finishes; a rival's warriors kill it if they catch it. Your capital is a
town. You raise an army because a rival's is on your fields, and a capital falls. On grass, a
rival herds, its lines reach further than yours, and its horsemen arrive before you expect
them. On the Tests screen you watch any rule run on a map small enough to follow.

Then the retro, and M6: beer, then knowing things.
