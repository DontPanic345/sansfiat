# M5: A loop that closes

What the M4 retro ([m4-retro.md](m4-retro.md)) and your notes on the game loop lead to. M4
built the economy and the rival, and left a game decided at the pick. The loop doesn't close:
a civ can't expand, and an army can't reach a rival fed. Both come from one fact: the capital
is the only place a civ has a store. Rival capitals are 11 to 18 steps apart on a small map,
a line costs more with every step, and 363 of 1,243 army-turns outside the ring are hungry.

M5 adds one thing, the settlement: a market that doesn't fade, a store the levy fills, and
land around it. A signpost is how you get one, a claim holds by joining one, and a line ends
at the nearest. Then goods that aren't eaten, and units that trade.

The Relay changes that go with it are in [m5-relay.md](m5-relay.md). Every piece runs whole on
Fable: that is M5's experiment. The scores are in [`docs/process.md`](../process.md).

## Your answers

The pieces assume these; change one and the pieces change with it.

1. **Land is held by being claimed, and lost by being walked on.** The guard and the militia
   go. A capital falls when a rival army stands on it with no army of its own there. L.
2. **A clan whose home is on your land is in your civ, that turn.** Off your land, clans are
   won at festivals as now. J.
3. **A settlement is the outpost.** No separate fort or cache in M5: a settlement stocks
   itself by its levy, and a line ends there. If settlements don't give an army its reach, a
   cache is the next piece. R.
4. **Salt and obsidian are the goods with a use.** A good is worth something only because
   someone wants it (`vision.md`). Wood and stone wait for walls; horses, sheep and goats
   arrive with herds in M6. G.
5. **The clock is closed as built.** The turn stays a year and distance isn't rescaled.
6. **Herds, pack animals, the page played and tests you can watch move to M6.**

## 1. Expand and reach

### L. Land without a guard

**Goal:** A civ's land is its claim, held with nobody standing on it and lost to a rival army
that walks onto it, so land and capitals change hands. The guard and the militia are gone from
the rules, the planner, the page and the text view. The question is doubt: with every civ on
the AI, do games end before the limit and do capitals fall? On main 0 of 7 end early, 0
capitals fall and 14 tiles are taken; the control is main's sweep.

**Whole:** fable medium

**Play:** sonnet

**Shape:** Clay and Kiln: what holds land is unsettled until it runs on the retro maps, and
the planner, page and view follow it.

**Design:** docs/design/war.md, docs/design/founding.md, docs/design/vision.md,
docs/design/page.md

**Decided:**

- A claimed tile joined to the capital is the civ's land. A rival army that ends its turn on
  it takes it, and with it any part of the claim no longer joined to the capital.
- No guard and no militia: neither is raised, shown, fed or counted. Clans don't defend their
  homes.
- A capital falls when a rival army stands on it and no army of its civ does.
- Only armies fight. The planner holds land with armies and reads what an army costs to feed.
- A fight among three civs on one tile is a rule, not a panic (#291).
- The view says what a claim order does to the claim and names its tiles.

**In scope:** `claim.rs`, the fight, the planner, the page's guard row and the text view's
guard lines, the judge's guard figures, tests on the stage, the ledger lines the guard had.

**Out of scope:** new unit kinds; walls; any change to how armies are fed.

### J. Joining, made plain

**Goal:** A clan whose home is on a civ's land is that civ's clan from that turn: it pays the
levy, can be raised from, and counts to Standing. The feed says which clan joined which civ
and why, and the map draws every clan in its civ's colour. A clan whose home tile is taken
changes civ with the tile. The question is motion: clans that change civ per hundred turns on
the retro maps, and the turns from a home coming onto a civ's land to the clan joining, about
eleven on main; the control is main's sweep after L.

**Whole:** fable medium

**Play:** sonnet

**Shape:** Easel and Canvas: the rule is one line, and what it does to gifts and Standing is
read on the maps.

**Design:** docs/design/clans.md, docs/design/founding.md, docs/design/war.md,
docs/design/page.md

**Decided:**

- A home on a civ's land makes the clan that civ's, with no leaning to wait for.
- A clan whose home is on nobody's land leans and joins by gifts and trade, as now.
- A clan leaves a civ when its home is no longer on that civ's land and it leans elsewhere.
- The feed line names the clan, the civ and the reason: land, gift or trade.
- The clan panel says whose the clan is and what it pays.

**In scope:** `leaning.rs`, `claim.rs`, the levy, the feed, the map's clan layer, the clan
panel, the text view.

**Out of scope:** new ways to win clans; any change to gifts.

**After:** #L

### S. Signposts and settlements

**Goal:** The player puts a signpost on a tile, and clans of the civ that divide or move on
weigh it as a site: the nearer and the richer, the likelier they go. The first clan to settle
there founds a settlement: a market that doesn't fade and a store its levy fills. A claim
holds where it joins the capital or a settlement. The planner places signposts. The question
is Standing: settlements a civ has at +100 and +200, and its Standing against the same civ
with no signposts; the control is main's sweep after J.

**Whole:** fable medium

**Play:** sonnet

**Shape:** Clay, Kiln and Glaze: the settlement is the clay, who walks to a signpost the
kiln, and how far a signpost draws is tuned on the maps.

**Design:** docs/design/founding.md, docs/design/clans.md, docs/design/farming.md,
docs/design/page.md

**Decided:**

- A signpost is an order naming any tile a clan can live on. A civ keeps a few at a time, and
  removes one by order.
- No settler unit. A daughter clan, or a clan moving on, chooses the signpost over its own
  best site by chance, weighted by the walk and the tile's worth. A clan that can't get there
  doesn't try.
- A clan that settles at a signpost founds the settlement. The signpost goes.
- A settlement has the capital's market and store rules: the market never fades, clans that
  walk to it pay its levy into its store, and the store spoils and has a cap.
- A settlement's market counts for the town rule, as the capital's does.
- A claim is every claimed tile joined to the capital or a settlement. A settlement a rival
  army stands on, with no army of its civ there, is taken with its store.
- The page draws signposts and settlements, and the settlement's panel shows its store, its
  clans and its land.

**In scope:** a `settle.rs`, `dividing.rs`, `clans.rs`' move, `claim.rs`, `civs.rs`' levy and
store, the town rule, the planner, the order, the page and the text view, stage tests.

**Out of scope:** paying clans to go; cities and walls; more than one capital; a settlement
that founds its own civ.

**After:** #J

### R. Supply from any store

**Goal:** A unit's line ends at the nearest store of its civ, capital or settlement, and the
ring is every tile a line from any of them feeds. An army marches from settlement to
settlement and arrives fed. The planner marches on a rival once a store is in reach of it.
The question is doubt: hungry army-turns outside the capital's ring, 363 of 1,243 on main,
and games that end before the limit; the control is main's sweep after S.

**Whole:** fable medium

**Play:** sonnet

**Shape:** Easel and Canvas: the rule is one line; what it does to reach, and when the
planner marches, is tuned against the judge.

**Design:** docs/design/supply.md, docs/design/war.md, docs/design/founding.md

**Decided:**

- A line runs to the store that feeds the unit for the least labour. It draws on that store
  alone.
- A unit is raised at any store, from the clans that go to its market, with a pack from it.
- The ring is the union of every store's ring, and the page draws it so.
- A cut line looks for another store before the unit goes hungry.
- The view names the store a unit draws on.

**In scope:** `units/line.rs`, `forage.rs`, `muster.rs`, `judge_ring.rs`, the planner, the
ring layer, the unit panel, stage tests.

**Out of scope:** caches an army leaves; porters; pack animals; moving food between stores.

**After:** #S

### C. Scouts that finish, and can die

**Goal:** A scout eats at a store: when its pack runs low it walks to the nearest store of
its civ, fills it and walks back to its errand, and it doesn't wait on rich ground. An army
that meets a rival's scout on its tile kills it, and a scout can't enter or leave a tile in a
rival army's zone. A route a scout finishes across a range is a way through: an army marches
it, a line runs along it, and a clan walking to a signpost takes it. The question is
decisions: does a led scout's errand finish, and do an army and a settling clan then cross
the range by its route? On small 61288 the route from Red's capital to Gold's stands
unfinished from +10 to +200 (`relay/retro/m4/game/scout-61288.txt`); the control is that game
on main.

**Whole:** fable medium

**Play:** sonnet

**Shape:** Easel and Canvas: the rules are settled; how far a scout gets on a pack, and when
it turns for home, is tuned on the retro maps.

**Design:** docs/design/scouting.md, docs/design/supply.md, docs/design/war.md,
docs/design/clans.md

**Decided:**

- A mountain tile a scout has bridged is walked by every actor at its trail cost: a clan, an
  army and a line, as well as the scout.

- A clan weighs a signpost by the cheapest path it can walk, a scout's pass included, so a
  signpost past a range draws clans once the pass is open.

- The pass's test is a built map on the stage: a range with one crossing, a scout that opens
  it, an army that marches through and a clan that settles on the far side, each by a named
  turn.

- A scout's food is its pack, filled at a store. Foraging on an errand goes.

- A scout's line in the view and its panel say what it is doing this turn: searching, walking
  to a store, walking back, or trapped.

- A rival army on a scout's tile kills the scout. A scout in a rival's zone stays where it is
  until the zone lifts or the army leaves.

- The scout's test is a built map on the stage: a known route, a pack that needs one trip to
  a store, and the errand done by a named turn.

**In scope:** `scout.rs` and its search, the scout's supply, the fight's scout rule, the zone,
`land.rs`' move costs over a bridged mountain, the clan's walk to a signpost, the panel and
view lines, stage tests.

**Out of scope:** AI orders for scouts; new scout orders.

**After:** #R

## 3. Exploit and exchange

### G. Goods

**Goal:** Two goods that aren't eaten come from fixed sources on the map and are wanted: salt,
which slows a store's spoiling, and obsidian, which raises a warrior's strength. Clans that
hold a source gather it as a job and trade it at their festival. A civ's store takes both by
its levy. The question is motion: the share of festivals where salt or obsidian trades, and
how far from its source each is found at +200; the control is the sweep with no sources.

**Whole:** fable medium

**Play:** sonnet

**Shape:** Clay, Kiln and Glaze: the goods table is the clay, sources and gathering the kiln,
and what a good is worth against food only works together.

**Design:** docs/design/goods.md (to draft), docs/design/actors.md, docs/design/labour.md,
docs/design/map.md, docs/design/war.md

**Decided:**

- A `Good` row carries weight, spoil, whether it is eaten, and its use. Eating, spoiling,
  `total` and the market's worth read it.
- A source is a tile feature: salt on some coast and marsh tiles, obsidian on a few hills.
  Gathering it is a job in the clan's labour split.
- Salt in a store slows that store's spoiling and is used up doing it. Obsidian in the store
  an army is raised from raises its warriors' strength and is used up at the raising.
- A clan wants salt by what its store loses to spoiling; a civ wants both.
- The table has no row for a good without a use.
- The page draws sources, and a store's panel lists every good.

**In scope:** the goods table, `map.rs`' sources, the labour split, `market.rs`' worth, the
levy, spoiling, the fight's strength, the planner, the page.

**Out of scope:** wood, stone, horses, sheep and goats; crafts; beer; traders.

**After:** #C

### X. Exchange

**Goal:** A unit trades at any market it stands on, a rival's excepted, giving what its pack
holds for food. Selecting a settlement and a good draws where that good came from: the source
and each market it passed through. The judge reports each good's share of all trades, so a
good that comes to stand on one side of most of them shows. The question is decisions and
motion: army-turns fed at a market that isn't the civ's, and the largest share any one good
has of trades at +200; the control is main's sweep after G.

**Whole:** fable medium

**Play:** sonnet

**Shape:** Clay and Kiln: how a good passes from one market to the next is unsettled until
it runs.

**Design:** docs/design/goods.md, docs/design/actors.md, docs/design/supply.md,
docs/design/clans.md, docs/design/page.md

**Decided:**

- A unit is a row in the market's clearing, as a clan is. It posts what its pack holds beyond
  food and takes food.
- A unit's pack is filled with salt at its store when the player orders it.
- A good passes between markets: the designer settles whether a clan carries light goods to a
  second festival or stores trade along the lines between settlements, by which moves
  obsidian further on the retro maps.
- Every unit of a good in a store remembers the market it last traded at, so the page draws
  its chain one good at a time.
- The judge's trade group: each good's share of trades by count and by worth, each turn.

**In scope:** `market.rs`, `markets.rs`, the unit's pack, the order, the flow layer on the
page, the judge's trade group.

**Out of scope:** traders as actors; partnerships; any good declared to be money.

**After:** #G

## 4. Order and cost

L, J, S, R, C, your play, G, X, one at a time: each `After` holds a piece until the one before
has merged. A whole piece starts only on a fresh 5-hour window.

The only measure of a whole piece on Fable is M4's R: $28 to $32 at list, 140 to 160 5-hour
points and two windows. Seven such pieces are about $210 and fourteen windows. J and C are
smaller than that; S and X are larger. The retro checks this: what a window buys is the
milestone's question about Relay.

## 5. What you play at the end

A small map where your land is yours until someone walks onto it. You put a signpost toward
the salt on the coast, and a daughter clan walks there and settles. Its people are yours, in
your colour. You raise warriors at the settlement, with obsidian points, and march on a
rival whose own settlement feeds the army coming the other way. Past your last store your
army buys food at a festival with the salt in its pack. A capital falls. You click your
capital, pick obsidian, and see the hills it came from and the markets between.

Then the retro, and M6: herds and pack animals, then beer and knowing things.
