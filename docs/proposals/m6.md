# M6: A claim in doubt, and herds

What the M5 retro ([m5-retro.md](m5-retro.md)) leads to. No notes from you came in the 72 hours
(`relay/retro/m5/owner.md`), so every piece here comes from the retro's angles. M5 closed the
loop in the AI's games: capitals fall, and a game ends by conquest. A player's game is still
open. Two rules from the view's own advice win it: keep four warriors home, and send the claim
the Reach line names every turn. That player ends at 89.5%, 51.7% and 51.9% where the AI ends
at 33.8%, 26.7% and 16.3% (`relay/retro/m5/game.md` §2). The planner's claim has a bound and
the player's has none. And a third of the pick's cards lose by +6, because a civ founds with no
army.

So M6 starts there: one bound on every claim, and an opening a civ can survive. Then the clay
that herds, beer and trade stand on, the proofs put where you can watch them, herds rebuilt
from H's spec, pack animals, and the page played. Beer and trade come after those. Knacks move
to M7.

The Relay changes that go with it are in [m6-relay.md](m6-relay.md). Every piece runs stepped,
with an Opus spec. The scores are in [`docs/process.md`](../process.md).

## Your answers

These are the retro's six questions, answered as the pieces assume. Change one and the pieces
change with it.

1. **What bounds a claim?** One rule for every civ, player or AI: a claim grows by at most a
   set number of tiles a turn. The Reach line names what fits. The planner's `PLAN.widen`
   becomes that number. A claimed tile still costs nothing to hold. W.
2. **Should the opening be survivable?** Yes. A civ founds with a few warriors from its founding
   clan, enough that small 3's Gold holds past +10 on the AI. The pick's card and the first view
   say which rival army can reach the capital, and in how many turns. This ends `founding.md`'s
   #152 line ("its scout alone: no army"). O.
3. **Does M6 hold to a list?** Yes: the nine pieces below, in this order. A piece you open
   partway through goes after them unless you say otherwise. Knacks move to M7, where the camel
   needs them.
4. **Where does a rule's proof live?** A rule a player sees is proved on the stage. One Ford
   piece moves the claim, the settlement's store and the line to the nearest store there, and
   from then on each piece proves its player-facing rule on the stage. The rest stay in Rust. T.
5. **Is crowding the right size?** Not yet known. The judge reports deaths by crowding beside
   starving, in W, and the rule stays until a map shows it's wrong.
6. **Relay on Codex (#503).** Decide it before M6's first piece opens. `m6-relay.md` holds the
   crew's fixes. If you take #503, its vendor plan goes in as its own section and changes the
   pacing and the costs below.

## 1. The claim and the opening

### W. A claim that grows by a turn's reach

**Goal:** Every civ's claim, the player's and the AI's, grows by at most a set number of tiles a
turn. The Reach line names the claim that fits this turn. The judge plays the two-rule Reach
player (`relay/retro/m5/game/reach.py`) beside the AI and idle controls on every map. The
question is doubt and Standing. On main, the Reach player ends at 89.5% on small 2, 51.7% on
small 6 and 51.9% on medium 8, where the AI on the same civ ends at 33.8%, 26.7% and 16.3%. Its
games have 0 lead changes, and on medium 8 no rival takes a tile in 250 turns. The control is
main's sweep, with the Reach player run on main.

**Spec:** opus medium

**Spec-review:** opus medium

**Play:** sonnet

**Shape:** Easel and Canvas: the rule is one number; how big it is gets tuned against the Reach
player and the AI together.

**Design:** docs/design/war.md, docs/design/founding.md, docs/design/vision.md,
docs/design/page.md

**Decided:**

- The bound counts tiles a claim adds in a turn, for every civ. Letting a tile go is free. A
  tile an army takes from a rival is the army's and doesn't count.
- A claim order that names more tiles than the bound allows takes the first ones it names, up
  to the bound. The view says how many were left for next turn.
- `PLAN.widen` becomes the shared bound, in `data/`, and the planner reads it as the player
  does. `PLAN.hold` stays as the planner's own care.
- The bound is tuned so that the Reach player still beats the AI on its civ, but its games are
  in doubt: a rival passes it, or takes its land, on at least some maps.
- The Reach line names the nearest claim that fits this turn's bound.
- `sweep --judge` runs the Reach player as a fourth control. Each civ's row gives its Standing
  and place beside the AI's, idle's and the piece's.
- The judge reports deaths by crowding beside starving, on every map (question 5).

**In scope:** `claim.rs`, the claim order and its refusal lines, the planner's widen, the Reach
line on the page and in the text view, the judge's Reach control and crowding figure, a stage
test of the bound.

**Out of scope:** a cost to hold a tile; any change to how land is taken or a capital falls;
the opening.

### O. An opening you can survive

**Goal:** A civ founds with a few warriors from its founding clan, standing on its capital. The
pick's card and the first view say which rival army can reach the capital first, and in how
many turns. The question is doubt. On main, 7 of 21 idle led civs fall by +6, small 3's Gold
falls at +2 on the AI, and small 6's Red falls at +4 after one raise. Capitals should still
fall later in the game: on main 6 capitals fall and 1 game of 7 ends by conquest. The control
is main's sweep after W.

**Spec:** opus medium

**Spec-review:** opus medium

**Play:** sonnet

**Shape:** Easel and Canvas: the rule is one line; how many warriors is tuned on the retro maps.

**Design:** docs/design/founding.md, docs/design/war.md, docs/design/page.md

**Decided:**

- At the pick each civ's capital holds an army raised from its founding clan. The clan pays its
  people for it, and the army's pack comes from the store, as a raise does. How many warriors
  is one number in `data/`, tuned so that small 3's Gold holds past +10 on the AI. The scout
  still founds as now.
- Every civ founds with the same rule: the AI's civs too.
- The pick's card names the nearest rival army by the turns it needs to reach the capital, and
  so does the first view's Capital line.
- This ends `founding.md`'s #152 line ("no army and no guard"). The review replaces it.

**In scope:** founding, the pick's card, the Capital line, the planner's first turns if it
needs them, a stage test of the opening.

**Out of scope:** a truce or a turn before which armies can't march; walls; new unit kinds.

**After:** #W

## 2. The clay, and proofs you can watch

### C. Clay for herds, beer and trade

**Goal:** The engine gets the seams M6's goods stand on, with no rule changed. A clan's days are
a table of named jobs that `actors::split` divides. A settlement's store is an `Actor`, read by
one lookup from a tile to its store. `move` skips the source reap when a territory has no
source. `Actor::carry` reads the actor. The atlas detail moves out of `engine/CLAUDE.md`. The
question is health. Digests should be equal on the retro maps (after one save bump), and large
8 should run at no more than 0.45 of the turn before #156. Main is at 0.53, and the guard alone
gives 0.47 (`relay/retro/m5/architecture/guard.diff`, `guard.txt`). The control is main.

**Spec:** opus medium

**Spec-review:** opus medium

**Play:** none

**Shape:** Henry Ford: each seam is settled in `relay/retro/m5/architecture.md` §4, and the
digests are the proof.

**Design:** docs/design/actors.md, docs/design/labour.md, docs/design/founding.md

**Decided:**

- Jobs are a table of named jobs (gather, fields, dig), each a share, its tiles and its
  product. A new job is a row, not a wider tuple. H's `jobs.rs` (PR #418) is the start. The
  `SALT` branch goes into the table's data.
- A settlement holds an `Actor` with no people, as the civ's store does. The levy, the line, the
  pay and the clearing read a store through one lookup from a tile. The 29 capital-or-settlement
  branches go. `SAVE_VERSION` bumps once.
- The `move` guard from `guard.diff`, then a cheaper valuation in `clans::worth` if the turn is
  still over 0.45.
- `Actor::carry` reads the actor, so a pack animal can change what one actor carries.
- `engine/CLAUDE.md` keeps the map. The atlas cell sizes go beside the atlas.

**In scope:** `jobs.rs`, `actors.rs`, `settle.rs`, `civs.rs`, `markets.rs`' store lookup,
`clans::worth`, `engine/CLAUDE.md`, the pins the bump moves.

**Out of scope:** the clearing's rows (J); herds; any rule or number.

**After:** #O

## 3. Animals

### H. Herds

**Goal:** A herd is a store that walks and breeds. Clans on grass keep animals, which graze
their land, breed by what it carries, and are eaten or traded. Herding clans hold wide, thin
land. It is rebuilt on main, on C's jobs table, from #342's spec and probe (on branch
`relay/342`, `relay/specs/342/`). The question is Standing. On main, 11 of 21 civs don't farm,
and they gain +0.012 of Standing against no fields where farmers gain +0.049. Do civs on grass
gain against the no-herds control? The control is the sweep with herds off.

**Spec:** opus medium

**Spec-review:** opus medium

**Play:** sonnet

**Shape:** Clay, Kiln and Glaze: the herd on the actor is the clay, grazing and wide land the
kiln, and the numbers only work together.

**Design:** docs/design/animals.md, docs/design/clans.md, docs/design/actors.md,
docs/design/labour.md

**Decided:**

- #342's spec stands: animals are a good, the herd is the clan's breeding stock in its store,
  herding is a job row, and the herd grows by the logistic rule toward what the clan's days
  and grass carry. A clan farms or herds, not both. A herder holds land to a wider radius, and
  its herd goes where it goes.
- The notch weighs a herd by its steady yield, not this turn's increase (#342 spec item 8,
  probe p4).
- A herd starts from the game on the clan's tiles, and from the wild beyond them where that
  isn't enough.
- `clans.rs` splits when herds touch it.
- PR #418 closes, and #342 closes with it.
- The page draws herds, and the clan panel counts them.

**In scope:** the animals good, `herds.rs`, the herding job row, wide land, the judge's herds
group and no-herds control, the page and the text view, a stage test of a herd.

**Out of scope:** pack animals; horsemen; milk, wool and hides.

**After:** #T

**Spec:** opus medium

**Spec-review:** opus medium

**Design:** docs/design/animals.md, docs/design/supply.md, docs/design/war.md,

- #343 closes into this piece.

**In scope:** carrying, the ring, the horseman kind, the planner, the page.

**Out of scope:** the camel; chariots; cavalry tactics.

**After:** #H

## 4. The page, played

### P. The page, played

**Goal:** A session plays the page in Chrome for 100 turns, on small 61288 and on a map chosen

are the led civ's own. The question is decisions: orders a player gives per hundred turns, and
levers pulled more than once. In the check (`relay/retro/m5/play.md`), the gift gave 0.0 all
game, the store sat near 50, and the army, scout and guard levers showed no result. The control
is that check and the page play in #338.

**Spec:** opus medium

**Spec-review:** opus medium

**Play:** sonnet

**Shape:** Clay and Kiln: what's worst is found by playing, then fixed.

**Design:** docs/design/page.md, docs/design/founding.md, docs/design/clans.md,

**Decided:**

**Out of scope:** paid art; new levers; pillage, which waits for armies abroad.

## 5. Beer, and jobs and trade

### B. Beer

**Goal:** Beer is brewed from grain by a settled clan as a job, keeps a little, and isn't food
for hunger. A civ that gives beer at a market holds a feast there, which draws clans to that
market. The question is motion. On main, capital towns are 3 of 15 at +200, and 2 civ stores
hold salt or obsidian. The measures are capital towns, and clans at a civ's markets, at +200.
The control is the sweep with no beer.

**Spec:** opus medium

**Spec-review:** opus medium

**Play:** sonnet

**Shape:** Clay, Kiln and Glaze: the good is a row, brewing a job row, and what a feast draws is
tuned on the maps.

**Design:** docs/design/goods.md, docs/design/farming.md, docs/design/clans.md

**Decided:**

- Beer is a `Good` row made from grain. It spoils faster than grain and isn't eaten against
  hunger.
- Brewing is a row in C's jobs table, open to a clan with fields.
- A civ's gift may be beer. Beer given at a market counts for more than its weight in food
  toward a clan's leaning there. How much more is one number in `data/`.
- Clans weigh a market with a feast as a festival worth the walk.
- `goods.md` has no beer prose yet, so this list is beer's design until you write it, as S's
  was for settling.

**In scope:** the beer good, the brewing job row, the gift, the market's draw, the page and
the text view, the judge's beer figures.

**Out of scope:** work fed with beer (great works, M9); other crafts.

**After:** #P

### J. Jobs and trade

**Goal:** A clan's days split into named jobs, and trading is one of them: a route between two
markets. It carries the clan's spare wares out and what it values more back, and produces
nothing. A clan that trades with all its people is a trader. Its route is cut as a line is.
The question is motion. On main, 2 civ stores hold salt or obsidian, 7 armies carry obsidian,
and 21 army-turns in 1,400 are fed at a foreign market. The measures are how far each good is
found from its source at +200, and the share of trades that cross from one market to another.
The control is the sweep with trade off.

**Spec:** opus medium

**Spec-review:** opus medium

**Play:** sonnet

**Shape:** Clay, Kiln and Glaze: the clearing's rows are the clay, the trading job the kiln,
and what a route is worth is tuned on the maps.

**Design:** docs/design/clans.md, docs/design/actors.md, docs/design/goods.md,
docs/design/supply.md

**Decided:**

- The clearing builds every row one way: one row per owner, with what it brought, what it kept
  and its worth. A clan is listed in every market its routes reach, so a trader sits in two
  (`relay/retro/m5/architecture.md` §4.3). The clearing moves to its own file.
- Trading is a row in the jobs table. Its share follows what the route returns a day, like any
  job.
- A route is cut as a line is, by a rival's land across it.
- A clan's second festival is a trading route. This ends `goods.md`'s #356 clause that clans
  don't walk to a second festival. The review rewrites that line.

**In scope:** `markets.rs` and the clearing, the trading job, routes, the judge's trade group,
the flow layer on the page.

**Out of scope:** partnerships; any good declared to be money; traders as units.

**After:** #B

## 7. What you play at the end

A small map where your civ founds with warriors at its capital, and the card tells you Gold's
army is four turns away. You claim toward the clans the Reach line names, a few tiles a turn,
and Blue threads its claim into the gap. Your grassland clans take up herding and spread
thin across the hills. Your settlement's store gives at its own market, and the gift line says
who it reached. You raise horsemen, march them on pack animals to a rival's settlement, and
take it. At the capital your farmers brew, and the feast draws clans in. A trading clan walks
your salt to Blue's market and comes back with obsidian.

Then the retro, and M7: the wide world, with knacks and the camel.
