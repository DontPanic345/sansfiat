# M2

What the M1 retro ([m1-retro.md](m1-retro.md)) leads to: Relay changes that let the spec plan its
own spend, and five larger pieces. M1 now holds only the retro (#30). #28 and #29 are in the
[M2 milestone](https://github.com/DontPanic345/sansfiat/milestone/3).

## 1. Relay

These land as one hand-built change from a session here, before any piece opens.

### Spec-review looks where the spec didn't

- **The crew makes the steps after spec-review**, from `steps.json` as spec-review leaves it.
- **The spec doesn't brief or choose spec-review.** Its model and effort go on the piece, beside
  the spec's, each on its own line:

  ```
  **Spec:** opus medium, by coin
  **Spec-review:** opus low, by coin
  ```

  `pieceField` reads one field per line, so the two can't share one. The `steps.json` check
  moves to after spec-review.
- **`spec-review.md`** becomes:

  > Before you open the spec, read the piece, its design files and the code it will touch, and
  > note where you'd expect this piece to go wrong. Then read the spec, the briefs and
  > `steps.json`, and look for what the spec didn't consider: a design line it reads
  > differently, a case it doesn't cover, a number that can't pass on the current map, a brief
  > that fixes what its build should find by measuring, a step not worth its own session.
  > Fix them in the spec, the briefs and `steps.json`; the crew makes the steps after you.
  > Leave what the checks will tell a build in seconds.

- **The review gets no brief** either. `review.md` changes "Your brief says where to look
  hardest" to: read the piece and its design before the spec, and judge each place the builds
  departed from the spec against the design. The designer still picks the review's model.

### Bigger steps, and the how left to the build

`spec.md` gains:

- Cut steps by area, not by time. A session has room for several times what M1's had: the
  longest ran 23 minutes and peaked at 19% of context, and each new session reloads 100–180k
  tokens of the code.
- Where a step is judged by measuring or by eye, its brief says what it delivers, the proof,
  and what other steps rely on from it, and leaves the method and the starting numbers to the
  build. The spec plans the order, the seams and the tests; the build designs the inside.

### Shapes and Ladders

`relay/prompts/spec.md` holds the scores, the shapes and the ladder; `relay/prompts/planner.md`
holds the flow and the words. In short:

- The designer settles what reading and asking can, then arranges what's left in a shape:
  Henry Ford, Easel and Canvas, Clay and Kiln, or Magnum. The piece
  suggests a shape on its `Shape:` line, and the designer may change it, saying why.
- Each build step plays a part in its shape. `steps.json` carries it, and the crew records it
  on the step issue, the PR comment and the steps table.
- Five scores sum to 0–15 points, and the sum climbs the ladder from Haiku low to Opus medium,
  with Sonnet medium at 7–8. At 9–11 the part decides: `canvas` and `clay` go Opus low, the
  review takes a coin between Sonnet high and Opus low, and the rest go Sonnet high. Every coin
  is written into the line it decides; the crew only copies it.
  A `magnum` step is always Opus. Opus medium is the top, for steps and design alike.
- `rates.md` goes, and with it the estimate. M1's rates stay in `relay/retro/m1/cost.md`; each
  step records what it took, and the retro sets that against its scores.

### Design held fixed

The designer and spec-review run on Opus, one medium and one low, by a coin thrown at
planning. That holds through M2, as the control the changes to the steps are measured against.

### M2's challenge

In `relay/prompts/spec.md`, where the designer reads it:

- Each piece runs at least one step on Haiku, except a Magnum piece.
- Each piece runs at least one step off the ladder, and its `why` says what it expects to
  happen.

### What sessions write

- **The step issue's body is its brief as it was when the step was made**, with the model,
  effort, points and `why` on top, and links to the piece, the PR, the brief on the branch and
  `spec.md`. It isn't kept up as builds correct the brief; the file on the branch is.
- **No comments on step issues.** One line at the top of the body says how it went: done,
  $1.30, 13 minutes. An unfinished attempt's note replaces it. Blockers go on the piece.
- **The PR is where to read a piece.** One comment per step: summary, decisions, ideas, stats
  and a link to the step. The crew hands the review a steps table (step, model and effort,
  part, points, $ and minutes), and the review puts it at the top of the description it
  writes, since `review.md` rewrites the whole body.
- **Links on the piece**: its design files and this proposal. The crew adds the spec folder and
  the PR when it makes the PR.
- **`session.md`**: the summary is one paragraph for the later steps: what changed that they
  build on, and what they must know. Tables, maps and sweeps go in a file under
  `relay/specs/<n>/`. Decisions are the ones you might overrule.

### The board

The board is kept. It failed because the crew sets each field inside one `try` with Status last,
and an unrounded cost fails first. The fix:

- Each field is set on its own, Status first, and cost is rounded to cents.
- Fields: Status, Step, Model, Effort, Attempts, List $, Minutes, 5-hour and weekly points
  used. Tokens, Context and Compactions go.
- A text field, **Waiting on**: "5-hour usage, until 14:20", "weekly usage, until Thursday",
  "your answer". So the board says why nothing's moving. `setBoard` learns to write a text
  field.
- Views can come later: **Pieces**, a board by Status over `kind:piece`; and **Steps**, a table
  grouped by parent issue, summing List $ and points.
- The board is for watching. The retro reads the step blocks, which carry the same figures and
  outlive the artifacts too.

### The rest

- The crew records 5-hour and weekly usage before and after each step, in its block and on the
  board. The retro takes the median points per $ for each model; a step far above it ran while
  you were working too.
- The 5-hour stop stays at 50%, and the weekly ramp stays (`relay/README.md`, Pacing): a
  slower pace leaves time to play each piece.
- Cut draft PRs, `tokens`, and the prompt no-ops the retro lists.
- `spec.md`: a brief never asks for evidence in the result; it names a file. A design line
  that can't or shouldn't be built as written is a decision that starts "Design says …".
- `session.md` names Chrome among the installed tools; six sessions went looking for it.
- `engine/CLAUDE.md`'s Layout rows cut to one clause each.
- **`engine/www/play.mjs`**: one headless driver, with clicks by tile and small screenshots,
  named in `engine/CLAUDE.md`. B's proof and C's review both drive the page, and M1's sessions
  each wrote their own.
- The schedule stays at six-hourly. The triggers worked through M1: your comments and replies
  started runs, `/relay run` dispatched one, and closing a piece closed its steps. After the
  change lands, I run each path once (`/relay status`, `/relay run`, a dispatch) before opening
  a piece.
- `engine/check.sh` runs fmt, clippy, test, `build-web.sh`, `cross-build.sh` and
  `node www/check.mjs`. CI's engine job runs it, and `session.md` names it.

## 2. Pieces

M2 builds what's wanted from merge4 into the engine, on the new clan model, and archives the
prototypes. Six pieces, each with no budget, and each shape suggested at least once. Below
each title is the issue body as Relay will read it, in the order they run. #28 closes into B,
and C replaces #29's body.

The prototypes are tagged `prototype/<name>`, and each piece deletes the one it replaces.
Their owner notes are in `docs/notes/`.

### A. Clans live off their land and meet at festivals

**Goal:** The clan model the rest of the game stands on. A clan spreads its labour over every
tile it holds, so it holds only land worth working; land worked too hard wears down; each clan
walks one route, to its festival; and people move between clans that meet there. The pre-game
still ends in three centres on its own. `hex-economy/` is archived.

**Spec:** opus medium, by coin

**Spec-review:** opus low, by coin

**Shape:** Clay and Kiln: the clan's land and labour model is the core every later piece
stands on. Once it holds, the festival route, people moving and the page are settled work on
top of it.

**Design:** docs/design/clans.md

**Decided:**
- The round goes. A territory is a connected patch of tiles around home.
- Every route is costed as a walk there and back, year on year: a clan's walk to each tile it
  harvests, its festival route, and later the army's supply line.
- A clan divides its harvesting labour over all its tiles, and each tile's share pays its walk
  first. A clan adds a tile only if its total harvest rises, so a poor tile can lower it.
  Walking inside its territory costs a clan little, but enough that trails still form.
- A tile harvested past what it regrows is depleted: its cap falls, and recovers once
  it rests.
- A clan grows only on what it harvests beyond its need. One that just meets its need holds,
  so the pre-game settles on a light population the land carries without strain.
- Taming rises a little each turn a tile's grain is held, and keeps rising through the main
  game; a tile's grain grows with it. Unheld grain goes wild again. A centre needs tamed grain
  in reach.
- A festival is a clan's market, run by the market algorithm. Food is too heavy to carry far,
  so clans trade little there but people.
- Each clan has one route, to one festival. It changes festival when another is worth more.
  If none is in reach, or the ones in reach aren't worth going to, a new festival forms, and a
  clan may hold one on its own.
- Clans at one festival trade people: starving clans shed people to growing ones, so a growing
  clan there grows faster and a starving one shrinks faster than it would alone.
- A clan too small to stand alone merges into another at its festival, or its people spread
  across the clans there.
- Trails wear where routes are walked year on year.
- The pre-game ends as now: three centres.
- `sansfiat sweep` reports, per map, when the pre-game ends, and the clans, people, depleted
  tiles and people moved at festivals over time, so it shows whether people level off.

**In scope:** territory, divided labour, depletion, the festival route, people moving between
clans, small clans, trails, the page's territory and routes, the sweep, tests, archiving
`hex-economy/` and `hex4x/`.

**Out of scope:** work besides harvesting (traders, crafters), more than one festival per clan,
anything civs do.

### B. A civ's army lives in its camp

**Goal:** The game moves from units walking about to an army moving its base camp, and a civ
can feed the army it raises. The warriors belong to the civ, fed from its store, which the
civ's clans pay into, and supplied along a line from the capital. So an army costs the clans
the people, the food and the carrying. On the retro's seven maps, with every civ on the AI
raising a camp at the pick, every civ still has one 200 turns later. Warriors stop walking
about on their own; `supply-lines/` is archived. Closes #28
and #29's supply half.

**Spec:** opus low, by coin

**Spec-review:** opus medium, by coin

**Shape:** Easel and Canvas: the camp, its stock, the line and foraging are groundwork. Whether
a civ can keep an army, and how far a line feels like it reaches, is the painting.

**Design:** docs/design/supply.md, docs/design/founding.md, docs/design/clans.md

**Decided:**
- At founding, each centre's biggest clan becomes the civ, and its home tile the capital. The
  centre's festival becomes the capital's market there.
- An army is a camp of warriors drawn from the civ's clans, belonging to the civ. The player
  moves the camp. Each civ starts with a small camp; disbanding sends warriors to the civ's
  own clan.
- Every unit carries a pack and eats from it; the camp's pack is its stock.
- Civ clans pay a levy into the store when they walk to the capital's market, the civ's own
  clan included. How much it takes is tuning, and it slows the clans' growth.
- The line runs from the camp to the capital, along the cheapest path. Its cost is the walk
  there and back over a year, and what it carries each turn is the labour put on it over that
  cost. The camp pays the labour.
- The civ's store starts with the boon year's surplus, which stocks the first camp.
- The camp forages on its own or unclaimed land, and pillages a rival's. The tile yields
  nothing for a while, and clans harvest it too.
- Auto-forage on, and auto-supply on to make up the shortfall.
- `sansfiat sweep` answers the goal, and runs once with no army as the control.
- The review plays a camp off its line and back through the page.

**In scope:** the camp, its stock and line, foraging and pillaging, the switches, raising and
disbanding, the line on the page, the sweep, events, tests, archiving.

**Out of scope:** fighting, holding ground and cutting lines (F), porters and caches, roads,
pack animals, goods other than food on a line, more than one camp per civ.

**After:** #A

### D. Clans are the prize

**Goal:** A civ grows by drawing clans to it. The civ goes to its festival as a clan does: it
posts what the store holds beyond what it keeps, and can give food. Clans that trade there and
are fed by it lean towards the civ, and in time join it. The player has a lever to pull
against the AI civs.

**Spec:** opus medium, by coin

**Spec-review:** opus low, by coin

**Shape:** Easel and Canvas: posting, gifts and leaning are groundwork. Whether joining is a
lever worth pulling is the painting.

**Design:** docs/design/founding.md, docs/design/clans.md, merge4/docs/DESIGN.md ("Clans are the
prize")

**Decided:**
- Leaning comes from trade at the civ's festival and from food given, and fades.
- A gift buys less leaning the more is given, and a starving clan values it more.
- A clan joins the civ it leans to most once its leaning is strong enough. Its daughters
  stay with the civ.
- The civ keeps back what its camp needs, and the player can change what it keeps.
- The AI civs post, give and draw clans by the same rules.
- `sansfiat sweep` reports clans joined per civ, and runs once with gifts off.

**In scope:** posting, gifts, leaning, joining, the civ panel's leaning clans, the sweep,
events, tests.

**Out of scope:** more cities, materials and crafts, treaties, clans leaving a civ.

**After:** #A

### C. Scouts open the country

**Goal:** Every rough tile hides what a trail could make it cost, and a civ's scout finds and
builds the best route between two points the player picks on the page. Its trails cut the cost
of every route that crosses them, the clans' and the camp's alike, so scouting widens a civ's
reach. `scout/` is archived. Closes #29.

**Spec:** opus low, by coin

**Spec-review:** opus medium, by coin

**Shape:** Henry Ford: it's a port of `scout/`, its rules are settled, and it splits into the
map's hidden costs, the search and the page.

**Design:** docs/design/scouting.md

**Decided:**
- A scout is a small party sent from the camp or the capital, a unit like the camp. It forages
  along its route, so it seldom draws on its line.
- Hidden trail costs are fixed when the map is made.
- One trail per tile, worn or built; any trail nobody uses wears away.
- The search works outward from the scout, rather than back and forth along a range.
- Selecting a scout gives "start here" and "route to here" in the selection panel. The page
  shows known and possible routes with their costs; the feed carries route found, no route
  and trail built.
- The review plays a scout's route through the page only.

**In scope:** hidden costs, the scout, routes, built trails, decay, the route view, events,
tests, archiving.

**Out of scope:** fog, bridge span as a technology, alliances, several scouts on one route.

**After:** #B

### F. Armies hold ground

**Goal:** An army holds the ground it stands on, and a rival's supply line can't cross it. The
player paints a zone from the camp, the army's people spread out over it, and a tile is the
army's only while its people stand there. So a civ can cut a rival army off from home, or
guard its own line. `paint-army/` is archived.

**Spec:** opus low, by coin

**Spec-review:** opus medium, by coin

**Shape:** Magnum: holding ground and cutting lines are one rule seen from both sides, and
the zone, the spread and the line test change together as they're tuned.

**Design:** docs/design/supply.md

**Decided:**
- paint-army's painting: a connected zone grown from the camp, painted and unpainted on the
  page, which the army's people spread over a tile a turn.
- A held tile is the civ's land while people stand on it. A rival's line can't pass held
  land, and a camp whose line is cut off eats its stock, then forages.
- Who holds a tile two zones both reach is the designer's call. No fighting.
- The AI paints a zone that guards its own line.
- The review cuts a rival camp's line through the page and watches it go hungry.

**In scope:** painting, the spread, held land, cutting lines, the zone on the page, the AI's
zone, events, tests, archiving.

**Out of scope:** fighting, entrenchment, forts, sieges, capturing cities, stewards.

**After:** #B

### E. Reading the world

**Goal:** The page explains the game: who holds a tile, where each clan's routes go, where the
camp and its line stand, why a clan, festival, camp or civ did what it did, and what concerns
the player. An old save fails with a reason. `merge4/` is archived, the last prototype.

**Spec:** opus medium, by coin

**Spec-review:** opus low, by coin

**Shape:** Henry Ford: each view is settled, and they split by what they show.

**Design:** docs/design/clans.md, docs/design/supply.md, merge4/docs/DESIGN.md ("Making it
readable")

**Decided:**
- Every rule that acts on a clan, festival, camp or civ records a short reason, and the
  selection panel shows the latest few.
- Alerts for what concerns the player: hunger in the camp, a line cut off, a clan joining a
  rival, people leaving its clans.
- Hidden country (valleys, sealed valleys, necks) on a toggle.
- A save version.

**In scope:** territory and routes on the map, reasons, alerts, the toggle, the save version,
tests, archiving `merge4/`.

**Out of scope:** new rules.

**After:** #C, #D, #F

**Order:** A, then B and D, then C, then F, then E.

## 4. Later

- **A steward**, or reeve, collects a levy from a painted area around the civ, beyond its own
  clans, and the civ claims as territory the land it taxes and protects. A guard, perhaps the
  warrior again, protects the same area.
- **Farming.** Clans move labour from hunting and gathering to farming as grain tames, the
  nearby festival wants grain, and pressure shrinks their territory. Farmed land doesn't
  deplete as gathered land does. Irrigation tames grain further.
- More trading, and traders between festivals.
- Secondary resources.
- **An army is paid** in something taxed from the civ's people. It posts what it has and what
  it wants at the nearby market: food, and weapons too, so blacksmiths trade there.
