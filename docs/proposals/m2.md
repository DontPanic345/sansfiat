# M2

What the M1 retro ([m1-retro.md](m1-retro.md)) leads to: Relay changes that let the spec plan its
own spend, and four larger pieces. M1 now holds only the retro (#30). #28 and #29 are in the
[M2 milestone](https://github.com/DontPanic345/sansfiat/milestone/3).

## 1. Relay

These land as one hand-built change from a session here, before any piece opens.

### Spec-review looks where the spec didn't

- **The crew makes the steps after spec-review**, from `steps.json` as spec-review leaves it.
- **The spec doesn't brief or choose spec-review.** Its model and effort go on the piece, beside
  the spec's, each on its own line with its why:

  ```
  **Spec:** sonnet high, 9 points (…): why.
  **Spec-review:** sonnet medium: why.
  ```

  `pieceField` reads one field per line, so the two can't share one. The `steps.json` check
  moves to after spec-review.
- **`spec-review.md`** becomes:

  > Before you open the spec, read the piece, its design files and the code it will touch, and
  > note where you'd expect this piece to go wrong. Then read the spec, the briefs and
  > `steps.json`, and look for what the spec didn't consider: a design line it reads
  > differently, a case it doesn't cover, a number that can't pass on the current map, a brief
  > that fixes what its build should find by measuring, a step not worth its own session.
  > Fix them in the spec, the briefs and `steps.json`; the crew makes the steps after you.
  > Leave what the checks will tell a build in seconds.

- **The review gets no brief** either. `review.md` changes "Your brief says where to look
  hardest" to: read the piece and its design before the spec, and judge each place the builds
  departed from the spec against the design. The spec still picks the review's model.

### Bigger steps, and the how left to the build

`spec.md` gains:

- Cut steps by area, not by time. A session has room for several times what M1's had: the
  longest ran 23 minutes and peaked at 19% of context, and each new session reloads 100–180k
  tokens of the code.
- Where a step is judged by measuring or by eye, its brief says what it delivers, the proof,
  and what other steps rely on from it, and leaves the method and the starting numbers to the
  build. The spec plans the order, the seams and the tests; the build designs the inside.

### Scoring and model choice

The spec scores each step on five things, and writes the points in its `why`. Each is worth a
different amount, by how much a stronger model or more effort helps with it:

| | Low | Middle | High |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Open**: can the brief say what done is? | 0: exactly | 2: in part | 4: judged by eye, or by searching for what works |
| **New**: is there a pattern for it? | 0: in the code, or a port | 1: adapted from one | 3: nothing like it here |
| **Miss**: what catches a mistake? | 0: the checks | 1: a test it has to write | 3: only play |
| **Importance**: what rests on it? | 0: it'll be rewritten | 1: this milestone builds on it | 3: a foundation, costly to change later |
| **Reach**: what does it change? | 0: a module on its own | 1: a few callers | 2: what every turn runs |

The game is early and much of it will be rewritten, so Importance is mostly 0 or 1 for now.
The map generator is 0.

The points add up to 0–15, and the sum picks the model and effort together, with **Sonnet
medium as the datum**:

| Points | 0–2 | 3–4 | 5–6 | 7–8 | 9–11 | 12–15 |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Step | haiku low | haiku medium | haiku high | **sonnet medium** or opus low, by coin | sonnet high | opus medium |

At 7–8 the spec writes `coin`, and the crew throws it when it makes the step and records the
throw in the step's block. The retro then has the two compared on the same kind of work, with
nobody choosing which got which.

Haiku takes the lower half. It's more capable than anything in its training data shows, so the
ladder doesn't hold it to mechanical work.

**Opus high isn't on the ladder.** It's for creative work that matters: a step gets it only
when Open, New, Miss and Importance all score high, so no sum of smaller scores reaches it.

The spec can go against the ladder, except into Opus high, and says why. Size isn't scored; it
decides how steps are cut.

The spec adds its steps up from `relay/rates.md` and writes the piece's estimate in `spec.md`.
The Relay change writes `rates.md` from M1: Sonnet high and Opus high are measured, and the
rest are guesses the spec marks as guesses.
Nothing caps it; the review reports the spend against it, and the retro checks scores against
how each step went and retunes the points and the ladder.

### M2's experiment

`rates.md` holds M2's challenge, and the retro replaces it:

- Each piece runs at least one step on Haiku.
- Each piece runs at least one step off what its points give, and its `why` says what it
  expects to happen.

Piece D's spec runs on Haiku high, from its points, with a Sonnet spec-review reading it cold.
That tests Haiku on planning, not only on building.

### What sessions write

- **The step issue's body is its brief as it was when the step was made**, with the model,
  effort, points and `why` on top, and links to the piece, the PR, the brief on the branch and
  `spec.md`. It isn't kept up as builds correct the brief; the file on the branch is.
- **No comments on step issues.** One line at the top of the body says how it went: done,
  $1.30, 13 minutes. An unfinished attempt's note replaces it. Blockers go on the piece.
- **The PR is where to read a piece.** One comment per step: summary, decisions, ideas, stats
  and a link to the step. The crew hands the review a steps table (step, model and effort,
  points, estimate, $ and minutes), and the review puts it at the top of the description it
  writes, since `review.md` rewrites the whole body.
- **Links on the piece**: its design files and this proposal. The crew adds the spec folder and
  the PR when it makes the PR.
- **`session.md`**: the summary is one paragraph for the later steps: what changed that they
  build on, and what they must know. Tables, maps and sweeps go in a file under
  `relay/specs/<n>/`. Decisions are the ones you might overrule.

### The board

The board is kept. It failed because the crew sets each field inside one `try` with Status last,
and an unrounded cost fails first. The fix:

- Each field is set on its own, Status first, and cost is rounded to cents.
- Fields: Status, Step, Model, Effort, Attempts, List $, Minutes, 5-hour and weekly points
  used. Tokens, Context and Compactions go.
- A text field, **Waiting on**: "5-hour usage, until 14:20", "weekly usage, until Thursday",
  "your answer". So the board says why nothing's moving. `setBoard` learns to write a text
  field.
- Views can come later: **Pieces**, a board by Status over `kind:piece`; and **Steps**, a table
  grouped by parent issue, summing List $ and points.
- The board is for watching. The retro reads the step blocks, which carry the same figures and
  outlive the artifacts too.

### The rest

- The crew records 5-hour and weekly usage before and after each step, in its block and on the
  board. The retro takes the median points per $ for each model; a step far above it ran while
  you were working too.
- The 5-hour stop stays at 50%, and the weekly ramp stays (`relay/README.md`, Pacing): a
  slower pace leaves time to play each piece.
- Cut draft PRs, `tokens`, and the prompt no-ops the retro lists.
- `spec.md`: a brief never asks for evidence in the result; it names a file. A design line
  that can't or shouldn't be built as written is a decision that starts "Design says …".
- `session.md` names Chrome among the installed tools; six sessions went looking for it.
- `engine/CLAUDE.md`'s Layout rows cut to one clause each.
- **`engine/www/play.mjs`**: one headless driver, with clicks by tile and small screenshots,
  named in `engine/CLAUDE.md`. B's proof and C's review both drive the page, and M1's sessions
  each wrote their own.
- The schedule stays at six-hourly. The triggers worked through M1: your comments and replies
  started runs, `/relay run` dispatched one, and closing a piece closed its steps. After the
  change lands, I run each path once (`/relay status`, `/relay run`, a dispatch) before opening
  a piece.
- `engine/check.sh` runs fmt, clippy, test, `build-web.sh`, `cross-build.sh` and
  `node www/check.mjs`. CI's engine job runs it, and `session.md` names it.

## 2. Pieces

Four, each larger than M1's, with no budgets: each spec sets its own. Below each title is the
issue body as Relay will read it. A, B and D open as new issues; C replaces #29's body, and #28
closes into it.

The crew runs the oldest piece that isn't waiting on an `After:`, so A runs first, then C, and
B runs whenever they wait on you or on usage.

### A. A civ that can keep an army

**Goal:** A civ can feed what it fields. On the retro's seven maps, with all three civs on the
AI and each making a second warrior at the pick, at least 18 of 21 civs still have both 200
turns later. Today 14 of 21 lose their one warrior.

**Spec:** sonnet high, 9 points (Open 2, New 1, Miss 1, Importance 3, Reach 2): the store, the
levy and the clans meet here, and supply lines and the army build on it.

**Spec-review:** sonnet medium: it reads the spec cold against the clan rules and the sweep.

**Design:** docs/design/founding.md, docs/design/clans.md, merge4/docs/mechanics/economy.md
(drafted labour)

**Decided:**
- The capital works the tiles next to it with labour drafted from the civ's clans, up to a
  quarter of each clan that isn't starving. Drafted people eat but don't forage, so the store's
  income is food the clans don't gather. Design says drafted labour is out of scope; this
  brings it in.
- At founding, every unaligned clan whose round touches the civ's land also joins: one ring,
  not a chain. Design says only clans within the market's reach join.
- A turn a clan isn't starving takes one off its `hungry` count instead of zeroing it
  (`clans.rs:232`), so a penned clan that's short more often than not moves on.
- Units eat from the store, as now. Design gives them packs; those come with supply lines
  (#29).
- Each civ starts with one warrior. Its scout comes with #29.
- `sansfiat sweep --main N` reports each civ's store, units, clans and people N turns after the
  pick, and answers the goal. It also runs once with no levy, since civ clans shrank after
  founding on 3 of 5 maps while unaligned clans grew.

**In scope:** drafted labour on the capital's tiles, the joining ring, the hunger change, the
sweep, events, tests.

**Out of scope:** more cities, projects, joining after founding, packs and supply lines, the
scout.

### B. A natural map

**Goal:** A new map type, natural, whose land reads as real landforms: ranges along plate
edges, rain shadows and deserts where the wind puts them. It becomes the default, and today's
map is kept as closed country.

**Spec:** sonnet high, 10 points (Open 4, New 3, Miss 3, Importance 0, Reach 0): the most open
work in M2, but it will be rewritten, so it stays off Opus.

**Spec-review:** sonnet medium: it reads the generator plan against the checks every map
passes today.

**Design:** docs/design/map.md

**Decided:**
- natural is the default. `?map=closed-country` on the page, and the same on the CLI, gives
  today's.
- Plates: about a dozen, with drift. Ranges and hills rise where plates converge, rifts and
  inland seas open where they part, and coasts follow plate edges.
- Climate: temperature falls with latitude and height. Rain comes off the sea on one
  prevailing wind per map and drops on the windward side of ranges. Biomes come from
  temperature and rain together. Design puts climate beyond deserts and rain shadow out of
  scope; natural maps bring it in.
- Deserts reach the share `map.md` asks for. Today's come out about 12% against 20%.
- Rivers, valleys, necks, fertility and foods run on the new terrain. The checks every map
  passes today (land share, open sites, three centres reachable) hold on both types.
- Every number goes in one struct per map type in `data.rs`.
- The proof is play: the PR shows small, medium and large maps of both types side by side.

**In scope:** the natural generator, the map-type switch on the page and the CLI, the checks on
both types, tests.

**Out of scope:** seasons, rivers changing course, choosing a map's numbers on the page.

### C. Supply lines and the scout (#29, closing #28)

**Goal:** A unit's reach comes from its food, and a civ's scout widens it. A unit carries a
pack, refills it along a supply line from its capital, and lives off the land past the line's
end. A scout finds and improves routes over hard country, given by the player through the
page, and its trails make lines cheaper. A's sweep, rerun with packs and a scout, still meets
A's goal for warriors kept at home. `scout/` is archived.

**Spec:** opus low, off the ladder. It scores 9 (Open 2, New 1, Miss 3, Importance 1, Reach 2),
which is Sonnet high. M2 has no Opus step at low effort, and I expect it to plan this as well
as Sonnet high would, for less.

**Spec-review:** sonnet medium: it reads the line, forage and trail rules against the clans
that harvest and walk the same tiles.

**Design:** docs/design/supply.md, docs/design/scouting.md, docs/design/founding.md,
docs/design/clans.md

**Decided:**
- A pack holds 8 turns of food; a warrior eats 1 a turn and disbands after 3 hungry turns. A
  unit is made with a full pack from the store.
- A resupply costs 1 labour plus 1 for every 3 of line cost. A unit that can't pay it from a
  turn's labour is cut off. Rival land blocks a line; rival units don't cut one yet.
- Forage 2 labour and pillage 1 labour, for up to 4 food; the tile yields nothing for 5 turns.
- Auto-supply on, auto-forage off, both on leftover labour after any ordered move.
- No porters. Rivers don't carry supply.
- Each civ starts with a scout as well as its warrior. A scout is made, fed, moved and
  disbanded like any unit, and carries a pack.
- Hidden trail costs are fixed at map generation, on both map types; about 1 in 5 mountains
  can be crossed.
- One trail per tile, worn or built, at the tile's hidden cost; any unused trail wears away.
- A revealed mountain takes the scout's whole turn.
- The search works outward from the scout instead of ping-ponging along a range.
- Selecting a scout gives "start here" and "route to here" in the selection panel, beside move
  and disband. The page shows known and possible routes with their costs; the feed carries
  route found, no route and trail built.
- The review marches a warrior off its line and back, and plays a scout's route, through the
  page only.

**In scope:** pack, line, resupply, forage, pillage, both switches, the line on the page, the
panel's pack and buttons; hidden costs, routes, built trails, decay, the route view; events,
tests, archiving `scout/`.

**Out of scope:** porters, caches, roads, pack animals, goods on lines, rival units cutting
lines, fighting, fog, bridge-span technology, alliances, several scouts on one route.

**After:** #A

### D. Reading the map

**Goal:** The page explains the main game: who holds a tile, where a round goes, where the
hidden country is, and why a clan or unit did what it did.

**Spec:** haiku high, off the ladder. It scores 8 (Open 2, New 1, Miss 3, Importance 0,
Reach 2), which is Sonnet medium. Haiku plans it to test Haiku on planning, and I expect its
spec-review to find more than A's or C's.

**Spec-review:** sonnet medium: it reads a Haiku spec cold.

**Design:** docs/design/map.md, merge4/docs/DESIGN.md ("Making it readable")

**Decided:**
- Territory: every clan's round tiles are painted before any clan's edges, so a clan holds the
  tiles it stands on.
- Rounds are drawn along each leg's path. The engine keeps only leg costs, so it gives the page
  the paths too.
- A toggle for hidden country: valleys, sealed valleys and necks.
- Every rule that acts on a clan, market, civ or unit records a short reason, and the selection
  panel shows its last eight.
- The main game's top bar and feed: one People stat, market reach drawn by cost, the legend off
  the map, rivers readable at fit zoom. Starving is an event after 3 hungry turns, not each
  flip.
- A save version, so an old log fails with a reason rather than a crash.

**In scope:** the page's territory, rounds, toggle, reasons, top bar and feed; the engine's leg
paths and reasons; the save version; tests.

**Out of scope:** new rules, the pre-game's markets.

**After:** #29

**Not in M2:** the pre-game's markets, and #27 paint-army until its interview.
The levy still reaches the store for free; carrying it in comes after supply lines.

**Order:** the Relay change, then A, then C, then D, with B alongside. `#A` becomes A's number when it opens.
