# Settling and farming

A clan by the river has lived on wild grain for generations, and the grain has changed
under its hands: bigger heads, seeds that wait to be cut. One year the clan puts some of
its people to the patch instead of the hills: clearing, sowing what it kept back, weeding
through the summer, cutting in the autumn. The patch feeds them better than the hills did,
and the next year more of the clan works it and fewer go out. The clan stops moving. Its
store fills past the spring. Its neighbours come to its festival with hides and fruit and
go home with grain, and some of them stay. In a few generations the tile is a town: many
clans at one market, fields along the water, a store the whole district eats from in a bad
year, and a prize an army would march a long way to take.

## The farmer

- A **farmer** is a clan that spends some of its days on fields and the rest gathering. The
  split follows what pays: a clan's grain tames (`clans.md`), and as tamed grain yields more
  per day than the hills, more days go to the field. Nobody chooses to farm; a clan farms as
  far as farming feeds it.
- A **field** is a small part of a tile, worked hard. Its size is the days its people put
  into it (`labour.md`), not the tile. A field yields grain in proportion to the days spent
  on it and the ground's fertility, and far more per day than gathering the same grain
  wild.
- A field doesn't wear as gathered land does. Grain that is sown and cut comes back; what
  rests is the people, in winter.
- Fields take only ground that will have them: a tile's river-side and floodplain first.
  Irrigation widens that ground later (`../roadmap.md`, M10).
- Seed is the farmer's own affair: a field's yield is what is left to eat after it.

## The home choice

- A farmer works from home and lives at home, so the field is where home is. A gatherer's
  home follows the middle of its territory; a farmer's follows its fields.
- Grain walks badly (`labour.md`, Carrying). A clan a tile from the market spends a real
  share of its year carrying its surplus there and its wants back. So a farming clan near
  a festival drifts to it, or the festival forms where the farmers are: either way the
  field and the market come to stand on one tile.

## Towns

- A **town** is a tile where many clans live: a market, fields along the water, and homes
  on one tile. Clans join a town the way they move anywhere, because it feeds them better:
  the market is at the door, so a surplus is worth its whole weight.
- A town's clans are still clans: each eats, grows, divides and leans as ever, and each may
  farm or gather. A town has no rule of its own; it is what a tile looks like when the
  farmers and the market have found each other.
- A town's store is the stores of its clans, and the civ's store if the town is a capital.
  A settled civ feeds more people on less land than a gathering civ, and what it keeps is
  worth taking (`war.md`).

## What the player meets

- The civ panel shows how its people live: gathering, farming, in the army. The clan
  panel shows a farmer's fields.
- Fields are drawn on the map, and a town reads as a town: many hearths on one tile.
- The feed tells when a clan takes up fields and when a town forms.
- The player's levers are the ones they have: the claim that protects the fields, the levy
  that fills the store, the gift that draws clans to the town, the army that takes a
  rival's. Nobody orders a clan to farm.

## What it needs first

Labour across jobs on the actor (`../architecture.md`): an actor splits its days between
jobs before each job divides over its tiles. The day as the one unit (`labour.md`). Many
clans on one tile without the per-turn cost growing with them (`clans.md`, Scale).

## What it isn't

- No farm as a building, no field as a tile improvement the player buys. A field is work.
- No crops but grain yet. Herds are `animals.md`; beer is `goods.md`.
- No irrigation, canals or walls: M10.
- No good and bad years. Every year is the same.

## Decided

- Clans farm from the pick: before founding no clan farms, and after it every clan may, unaligned ones included. (#155)
- Field ground is the days a tile's fields take a turn: a floodplain takes 15, a river tile up to 7.5 by the river's size, both add, on any land; lakes and the coast take none. It comes from the map's rivers and floodplains, with no roll of its own. (#155)
- A field day yields grain by the tile's fertility and its tame past wild: nothing on wild grain, 1.2 food at a hearth of fertility 1, against a gathering day's 0.75. A clan sows only fields that yield. (#155)
- A clan's share of days on fields moves a tenth a turn toward the share that feeds it better, when the gain is worth it; a starving farmer drops a tenth a turn. A daughter starts at her mother's share, and a clan that moves on starts at none. (#155)
- A clan with a fifth of its days or more on fields is a farmer, and its home steps a tile a turn toward its busiest field; the civ's own clan stays on the capital. (#155)
- The harvest target caps fields and gathering together: a farmer that meets it from its fields gathers less land. (#155)
- A field tames its tile a step a turn faster, past the hearth too. (#155)
- The civ panel's Living row counts its clans' people as gathering or farming by their share of days, and every unit's people as in the army. (#155)
- A clan's fields may be on its home tile, whoever holds it, and on the tiles it claims. A tile's field ground is shared among the clans sowing it, by the days each sows; each plans on what the others sowed there last turn. (#156)
- A farmer's home makes one choice a turn: stay, step toward its busiest field, or step a tile toward its festival onto any land, whoever holds it. It steps when that feeds it better by 0.01 food a person, counting what its labour could reap past the store's cap. (#156)
- A tile reads as a town once three clans live on it and a festival stands there. The feed tells when one forms ("A town forms at (3, −2): 4 clans live by the festival"). (#156)
