# Technology

Nobody discovers the wheel in a tree. A civ gets better at the things it does by doing
them; now and then someone stumbles on something; and most of what a civ knows it learned
from the people it trades with, fights and lives beside. An advantage is real but it leaks,
so a civ that leads has to keep leading, and a civ that chooses to lag and copy is playing a
strategy, not losing.

## Knacks

- A **knack** is a way of doing one thing: spear-fighting, bow-making, foraging, field-work,
  herding, trail-building, salt-boiling. Each has a level, and a level is a small edge: a
  few percent more from that activity.
- There's no tree. No knack waits on another; what waits on what is in the world (bows need
  forest, herding needs herds).
- A civ's knacks belong to its clans and units together. A clan that joins brings what it
  knows.

## How a knack grows

- **Practice.** A turn spent doing the thing raises its knack a little. Archers in a
  skirmish raise bow-making; a clan on its fields raises field-work.
- **Luck.** Now and then a turn of practice jumps: a serendipitous find. Rare, and never
  chosen.
- **Contact.** At a festival two civs share, after a skirmish, and along a shared border, the
  lower knack drifts toward the higher. Trade spreads most, fighting next, living beside
  least.
- Nothing is spent to learn. Research isn't a button; it's a clan put to the thing.

## What it does

- A knack scales the thing it's a knack for: strength in a fight, the harvest from a tile,
  the labour a trail takes.
- The player sees knacks in the civ panel and in why a fight went as it did. The rest of the
  world's knowledge stays out of sight.

## What it needs first

Unit kinds and fighting (`war.md`); herds (`../roadmap.md`, M5), since the camel is a knack.

## What it isn't

- No tech tree, no eras, no research points.
- Not a race: lagging is a strategy because contact closes the gap.
