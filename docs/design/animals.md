# Animals

A draft from the roadmap, for the owner to correct before the herds piece opens.

On grass that grows little grain, a clan keeps animals instead of fields. In the spring its
herd is larger than in the autumn, and the clan walks with it to where the grass is. It holds
far more land than a farming clan and lives thinly on it. At the festival it trades animals
for grain, and a civ that has such clans has something its neighbours want.

Later the animals carry. A line of pack animals brings food further than people on foot can,
so an army reaches a country it couldn't before. A civ with herds raises horsemen, who arrive
sooner than anyone expects and need feeding when they get there.

## Rules

- A herd is a store that walks and breeds. It belongs to a clan, goes where the clan goes, and
  grows by what the land it grazes can carry.
- Grazing takes a clan's days, as fields and gathering do, and a clan chooses between them by
  what feeds it better.
- A herding clan holds wide, thin land and moves across it. A farmer stays, and doesn't herd.
- Animals are eaten and traded. They are the first good with a use beyond eating: they carry.
- A pack animal carries more than a person and eats grass on the way, so a walk with animals
  costs fewer days and a line reaches further.
- Horsemen are raised only by a civ whose clans have herds. They march further in a turn than
  warriors, and eat more.
- Everything an animal does is priced in days: the days it saves a walk and the days its
  keeping takes.
