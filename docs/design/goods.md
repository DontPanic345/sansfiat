# Goods

Drafted by piece #355 for the owner to rewrite: it says what the engine does, in the owner's
terms, and nothing the piece didn't decide.

Not everything in a store is eaten. Salt and obsidian come from fixed places on the map, are
light to carry and don't rot, and each is wanted for the one thing it does. A clan that sits
on a salt pan has something every full store wants; a civ with obsidian hills in its land
fields better warriors than one that has to trade for blades.

## The table

- Every good is a row: what it weighs, how fast it spoils, whether it is eaten, and its use.
- The foods are eaten and spoil. Only what is eaten feeds people, fills a pack and counts as
  a store's food.
- A good with no use has no row.

## Sources

- A source is a feature of its tile, fixed when the map is made: salt on some coast and marsh
  tiles, obsidian on a few hills. Salt is common; obsidian is rare.
- A clan that holds a source gathers it as a job, beside its gathering and its fields, and
  only up to what it cares to hold.
- It brings what it doesn't need to its festival and trades it for food.

## Salt

- Salt in a store slows that store's spoiling, and is used up doing it.
- A clan wants salt by what its store loses to spoiling: a clan with nothing put by wants none.

## Obsidian

- Obsidian in the store an army is raised from arms its warriors: an armed warrior is
  stronger in attack and defence. It is used up at the raising.
- In a clan's store it is tools: a tooled clan gathers more, and its tools wear out. A civ gets
  it by its levy and at its market.

## The civ

- A civ's store takes salt and obsidian by its levy with the food, and wants both at its
  market.

## On the page

- The map draws sources.
- A store's panel lists every good.

## What it isn't

- No wood, stone, horses, sheep or goats yet; no crafts, no beer, no traders. Traders come
  as a clan's job, carrying goods between two markets for what it gains (`roadmap.md`, M6).

## Decided

- Salt and obsidian weigh a fifth of food and don't spoil; a store's cap and a pack count food only. (#355)
- A small map has 12 salt sources and 5 obsidian, more by area. A holder gives a twentieth of its days to the source, a day giving three quarters of a unit of salt or a quarter of obsidian, and stops at half a unit a person. (#355, #356)
- A unit of salt keeps 10 food for a turn at half its spoiling and is used up. (#355)
- A warrior raised takes half a unit of obsidian from the store he is raised from and fights a quarter stronger; an army's armed share stays through its losses. (#355)
- At a festival a clan brings the obsidian past its own tools and the salt its own store won't use; a good nobody there wants isn't traded. (#355, #356)
- A civ's levy takes all the salt and obsidian its clans bring, whatever room its store has for food, up to four turns of salt for a full store and obsidian for eighty warriors; past that the levy leaves them with the clan and the store wants none at its market. (#355, #356)
- A tenth of a unit of obsidian tools a person, who gathers a fifth more; a tenth of a clan's tools wear out a turn, and a clan wants tools as it wants a food. (#356)
- A starving clan gives none of its days to a source: digging cost a clan its food. (#355)
- A good passes from one market to the next by a clan's festival, the levy into a store, and an army's pay from that store to the market its line ends at; clans don't walk to a second festival. (#356)
